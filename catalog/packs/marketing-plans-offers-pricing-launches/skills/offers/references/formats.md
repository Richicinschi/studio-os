# Offer shapes by business type

The same six parts get assembled differently depending on what is sold. Find the closest type, start there, adapt. The classic error is borrowing a shape from another type, such as pushing loud direct-response bonus piles onto a quiet professional service.

Choosing: ask who buys and how experienced they are, and what limits the business most (capacity for services, tiers for software, dates for courses). Match the scarcity form to that limit.

## Freelance or service work

- Core: a scoped engagement with a named deliverable and end date.
- Bonuses: templates and frameworks that leave the client self-sufficient afterward.
- Guarantee: pay for a first milestone, or a refund tied to the first deliverable.
- Scarcity: capacity, with the next opening date.
- Name: outcome or method.
- Price: fixed project fee with deposit, or monthly retainer.

Watch: unnamed services compete on price, named ones on position. Put in, out and optional in writing before work starts, because scope creep ruins offers.

Productizing: turn "I do consulting" into "the 6-Week Site Audit". It gets a name, fixed scope, fixed time, fixed price, the same extras each time, and a checkpoint (a week-three review, say). It raises perceived value, simplifies selling and produces repeatable case studies.

## Courses

- Core: curriculum plus delivery format.
- Bonuses: templates, examples, community access.
- Guarantee: refund gated on completion.
- Scarcity: enrollment close date for cohorts; price rise or bonus end for self-paced.
- Price: in full, or two to four installments.

Self-paced versus cohort: self-paced courses usually sit lower in price, use email sequences and bonus deadlines, and can afford a more generous refund. Cohorts sit higher, use a launch window with a real close date, and gate refunds on effort.

Watch: finishers are your proof, so plan a first win in week one. A close date followed by a quiet reopening is noticed. A guarantee with smart conditions sells well and rarely gets claimed.

## Coaching

- Core: sessions, written access between them, a defined target.
- Bonuses: library access, introductions, follow-up check-ins.
- Guarantee: an exit after the first sessions, or result-or-extension.
- Scarcity: a fixed number of places per quarter, or group size.
- Name: identity or outcome.
- Price: monthly or a fixed term.

One-to-one: scarcity is capacity, and the guarantee is a stop after early sessions. Group: scarcity is group size, there is a trial period, and the value often lies in the peers.

Watch: for groups, the room is the offer, so name the room. A sloppy intake hurts belief. Design for the renewal decision, not just the first purchase.

## Info products (guides, template packs, swipe files)

- Core: the asset and ongoing access.
- Bonuses: neighbouring assets, a walkthrough.
- Guarantee: generous and unconditional at this size.
- Scarcity: bonus end, scheduled price rise.
- Price: modest, paid in full.

Watch: the buyer opens it once, so the first minutes must feel solid. Ship a ten-minute "start here" walkthrough. Say what "lifetime access" means (your lifetime, the product's, until retired).

## High-ticket B2B

- Core: an engagement lasting several months, or a yearly contract.
- Extras: setup, staff training, hooking into existing systems, a named success contact.
- Guarantee: service levels, performance terms, or a gated pilot.
- Scarcity: quarter-end terms, onboarding capacity per quarter.
- Name: a stable plan name plus a named engagement type.
- Price: annual with periodic billing, often custom.

Watch: the offer must work for the champion, the budget holder and the skeptic at once. Your commercial terms (payment days, security review, contract paper) are part of the offer, and rigid terms lose deals. A short paid pilot lowers decision risk. The success contact counts toward perceived value.

## Agency retainer

- Core: monthly deliverables, a named team, a reporting rhythm.
- Bonuses: strategy sessions, audit credits, tool access.
- Guarantee: paid first month, or an exit clause after a set period.
- Scarcity: clients taken per vertical or quarter.
- Name: tier plus service line.
- Price: monthly, with a discount for a longer commitment.

Watch: slow starts lose to agencies that deliver something in week one. Clear action-focused reports reduce churn more than extra deliverables do. Design tier steps so the upgrade case is obvious.

## Self-serve software

- Core: tiered subscription with clear differences.
- Bonuses: onboarding help, templates, integrations.
- Guarantee: free trial, or annual plan with a refund window.
- Scarcity: founding price for the first N customers, or seasonal pushes.
- Price: monthly or annual, tied to a value metric.

Here tiers and value metric do more than bonuses and guarantees, so go to the pricing skill first and use this one as a supplement. Trial design is offer design: length, gated features, card required or not, automatic conversion. The annual-prepay discount is a major lever; improving the annual offer often helps more than tuning monthly.

## Direct response and paid traffic

- Core: the product with a clear payoff.
- Bonuses: a heavier stack, still honest.
- Guarantee: bolder risk reversal.
- Scarcity: genuine deadlines (launch window, evergreen with a hard close).
- Name: attention-grabbing.
- Price: single payment with a plan available.

Watch: cold audiences expect energy, and a hushed premium style converts poorly there. Build refunds into the margin math; if the margin cannot take a realistic refund rate, restructure. The opening seconds decide the rest. This shape needs direct-response skill; borrow it or hire it if you lack it. Never stretch it into the fake urgency the scarcity file rules out.
