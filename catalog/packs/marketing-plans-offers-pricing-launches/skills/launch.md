## When to use

Typical wordings: "plan the launch", "we ship next month", "should we do Product Hunt", "set up a waitlist", "announce this feature", "GTM plan", "give me a launch checklist". Anything where something is about to be released to people outside the team.

Neighbours:
- Marketing that continues after launch week: marketing-ideas (its tactics for directory launches and early-access referrals fit here too).
- The offer being launched (bonuses, guarantees, scarcity, naming): offers.
- Launch and onboarding emails: emails. Launch landing page tuning: cro. Psychology of waitlists and exclusivity: marketing-psychology. Comparison pages: programmatic-seo. Sales collateral for the launch: sales-enablement.

## How it works

1. Gather context. If a saved product marketing context file exists (`.agents/product-marketing.md`, or the older `product-marketing-context.md`), read it first and ask only what it lacks. Then ask: what is shipping (new product, big feature, small update); audience size and engagement; owned channels (list size, blog traffic, community); the deadline; what earlier launches taught; whether a directory launch is planned and how ready it is.
2. Treat launching as a habit. Every release is a chance to be noticed, so plan a run of moments (early hands, real feedback, a small splash at each stage) instead of one big day. Momentum compounds.
3. Sort channels into owned, rented and borrowed, and make all of them point back to owned. Pick one or two owned channels, one or two rented ones, and a short list of borrowed ones. Detail and examples: [channels](launch/channels.md).
4. Choose the stage you are in and run its actions: internal, alpha, beta, early access, full launch. Detail: [phases](launch/phases.md).
5. If a directory launch such as Product Hunt is on the table, weigh its costs first and prepare in three windows: before, on the day, after. Detail: [product hunt](launch/product-hunt.md).
6. Keep the work as a plan and drafts. Do not post, send or pay for anything yourself (emails, social posts, directory listings, ads, gifted products). Ask the owner first and state the cost.
7. Match the effort to the size of the release, because not every update deserves a campaign:
   - Major (new feature, overhaul): several channels together, with blog post, email, in-app message and social.
   - Medium (new integration, interface improvement): a targeted note to the relevant segment plus an in-app banner.
   - Minor (fixes, small tweaks): changelog and release notes only.
   Spread releases out rather than bundling all news into one drop, reuse whatever worked last time, and publish even small changelog entries: they show the product is alive, which helps retention and word of mouth.
8. Plan the weeks after the announcement as adoption work, since building on momentum is cheaper than restarting. Queue: an automated onboarding email series for new users; a mention in the next regular roundup email for people who missed the news; comparison pages against alternatives; dedicated sections about the new thing across the site; a no-code interactive demo so visitors can try before signing up. Each touchpoint should point back at the launch.
9. Hand over the checklist in [checklist](launch/checklist.md), adjusted to the stage and the channels chosen.

## Checks before done

- The plan names the stage, the date, the owned channel that catches all traffic, and one measurable goal (for example signups or activations).
- Every rented or borrowed tactic ends in a capture step (email signup or account creation).
- Directory launch costs and prep time are stated, or it is explicitly skipped.
- The checklist is attached and trimmed to what applies.
- Nothing was sent, posted or bought; each outward step is listed as waiting for the owner's word, with its cost.
