# Audit output layout

Use this skeleton for every audit.

## 1. Header

App name, store, listing link, audit date, brand tier with a one-line reason, overall score out of 100 and grade.

## 2. Score card

One row per dimension: score out of 10, letter (A for 9 or 10, B for 7 or 8, C for 5 or 6, D for 3 or 4, F for 1 or 2), and a one-line key issue. Last row: the weighted overall.

## 3. Three quick wins

Each takes under an hour. For every one give: the action, impact (high or medium), effort (under 15 minutes, 30 minutes or an hour), the current text, the exact replacement with its character count, and one sentence on why it helps.

## 4. Findings per dimension

- Title and subtitle: current text with characters used out of the limit, problems, a recommended replacement with count and rationale.
- Description: the first 3 lines as they stand, problems, keyword density (Google only, against the 2 to 3% aim), the main terms found with counts, valuable terms missing, a rewritten opening.
- Visuals: screenshot count and how many show in search, video yes or no, icon notes, feature graphic (Google), a screenshot-by-screenshot pass or issue note, specific fixes.
- Ratings and reviews: average and count, sentiment of recent reviews, complaint themes, whether the developer replies, actions.
- Metadata and freshness: last update and its age, languages, category, events or promotional content, actions.
- Conversion signals: pricing model, number of in-app purchases, Google download range, visible proof, actions.

## 5. Keyword table

Columns: keyword, why it matters, where to place it (title, subtitle, description or keyword field), priority. Note that without paid tools there is no search volume data, so the list rests on category analysis, rival metadata and meaning. Volume checks need a dedicated ASO tool, which the user chooses and pays for; ask before suggesting spend.

## 6. Competitor table (only if requested)

Rows: title keywords, rating, screenshots, video, description keywords, last update, overall score. One column for the user's app and one per rival.

## 7. Action plan

Group by timing: this week (quick wins), this month (medium effort), next quarter (large effort). Each line gives the action and the expected effect.

## 8. Limits of the audit

Always close with what cannot be measured from public pages: search volume and difficulty, past rank positions, download and revenue estimates, the hidden Apple keyword field, install conversion rate (owner console only), and earlier test results.
