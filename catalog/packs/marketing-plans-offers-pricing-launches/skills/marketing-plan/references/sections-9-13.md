# Sections 9 to 13: what to draft

## 9. 90-day roadmap

Tables, not prose; aim for one printed page. Every row has a move, a funnel stage and an owner. Four sprints:

- Weeks 1-2, Unblock: cheapest, most certain fixes; remove what is broken.
- Weeks 3-4, Foundation: base work such as domain consolidation, first content, first flows, first tests.
- Weeks 5-8, Velocity: cadences start, tests repeat, channels scale.
- Weeks 9-12, Compound: second-order moves and preparation for the 90-day review.

If nobody owns strategy, the first move is the hire (Manager or Lead title). If strategy exists but hands are missing, the first move is a contractor or small specialist agency (see team-model.md).

## 10. 12-month outlook

Open with a framing paragraph that states: the budget method used with its arithmetic (budget-math.md); the annual budget plus the experiment buffer; the year-end ARR goal as a forecast, not a promise; the growth pattern expected (linear with a stated monthly add, step-function, or layered curves); for venture-backed Series A and later, how the plan sits against the company's own past growth or why it chooses a slower path.

Then one block per quarter (about 250 to 400 words each):
- Months covered and the funding tier active (funding-tiers.md).
- One-sentence focus.
- 5 to 8 outcomes by quarter end.
- 3 to 5 KPI targets with numbers.
- Curve position: which channel, product and market curves are rising, flattening, or being staged next.

## 11. Marketing operations stack

Tables plus short prose. Parts: thesis (two paragraphs: a small team with the skill library and tool connections produces the output of a larger one); skills by funnel stage; tool connections by stage (existing versus the planner's layer); one concrete example; capability unlocks by funding tier; team and agency table.

Team table columns: function (growth = paid and outbound demand, product marketing = positioning and message, content = credibility and proof), owned by (internal strategic role), executed by (employee, contractor or agency). Details and the example rule are in ops-stack.md and team-model.md.

## 12. Tactical idea bank

Maps the full 139-idea library to funnel stages with a client-specific status. Parts: an intro (sections 4-8 say what will be done, this says what is possible); the status legend (Now in Q1, Q2, Q3+, Q4+, Skip with reason); six subsections (acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue, cross-cutting) each as a table of number, idea, client note; a summary of counts per stage, skipped count with rationale, the share of the surface the plan covers, and what that says about the stage. Tables can run past 150 rows. Method and base mapping are in idea-mapping.md.

## 13. Measurement, RACI, open decisions, appendix

- North star: one metric specific to the business model (measurement.md).
- Leading indicators table, one row per funnel stage.
- Review cadence: weekly, monthly, quarterly, who attends, what comes out.
- RACI table (responsible, accountable, consulted, informed) over the domains that apply, such as the plan itself, voice, build work in the product, lifecycle mail, search content, store listing, founder posting, events, ambassador program, B2B selling, price changes, the investor story and upcoming hires.
- Open decisions ranked by impact, each with name, impact and what it blocks. Unknown CAC leads.
- Appendix in three groups: documents in the shared repository, founder-authored context in the internal knowledge base, and working drafts not yet published (say how to obtain them). Only list what the team can open; no machine-specific paths.

## Output format

Single markdown file. H2 for each numbered section so a Notion paste keeps structure. Tables for anything compared (RACI, idea bank, stack). Refer across sections with the section sign and number. Typical length 8,000 to 12,000 words; shorter for early-stage clients with little surface area, longer when there is a long history to acknowledge. An empty section says so in a line rather than being padded.

## Is this section done

- 1: a stranger can state the growth thesis from it alone.
- 2: a new copywriter could follow the voice rules.
- 3: every in-flight item has an owner and a blocker.
- 4 to 8: each move names a skill and a tool.
- 9: every row has an owner.
- 10: every quarter names its funding tier.
- 11: at least one concrete proof of the stack.
- 12: skips have reasons.
- 13: the north star fits this company, not "ARR growth".
