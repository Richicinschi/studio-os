# Where to pitch

A starting set of places for software and SaaS coverage. Directories such as Product Hunt or BetaList are not press; for those use `directory-submissions`.

## Ground rules

- Pitch a person at the outlet, not the outlet. Use the discovery work in building-a-media-list.md.
- Tier says how hard the bar is, not how much effort to spend. A small niche outlet can be the best fit.
- A reporter's direct email beats a tip form.
- This list ages. Before any pitch, check the outlet still runs and the person is still there. Contact pages change, so look them up fresh; none are recorded here.

## Tech and startup press (tier 1)

High bar, high payoff. Examples: TechCrunch (funding, launches), The Verge (consumer tech, policy), Fast Company (innovation, design), VentureBeat (AI, enterprise, gaming), Ars Technica (deep tech, science, policy), The Information (paywalled scoops), Bloomberg and Reuters (business, finance), and the tech desks of the big business and national papers.

## SaaS and B2B (tier 1 to 2)

Less famous, often better returns for B2B. Examples: SaaStr (contributed posts), First Round Review (original operator frameworks only), research-backed blogs on metrics, product-led growth and pricing (the venture firm OpenView stopped investing in late 2023, so check that any such blog still publishes), product and growth newsletters (pitch the author with a sharp angle), and strategy analysis blogs where you engage with replies and do not pitch.

## AI and ML (tier 1 to 2)

The busiest beat, so the angle must be sharp. Examples: The Decoder (daily AI news), Import AI (research-backed angles), The Batch (industry analysis), MIT Technology Review (policy, ethics; slower and higher bar), the Hugging Face blog (open-source tools, contributed posts), Latent Space (practitioners).

## Developer and devtools

Examples: The New Stack (contributor programme), InfoQ (architecture, long technical pieces), DEV.to (self-published; earn credibility first), Hacker Noon (easy to publish, little signal), Console (curated weekly project list), DevTools FM (podcast guests).

## Business and marketing

Examples: Inc. (founder stories, contributor programme), Entrepreneur (volume, uneven quality), HBR.org (original research, long lead time), MarketingProfs (contributor-friendly), Marketing Brew (reporter-led, pitch directly), Search Engine Land and Search Engine Journal (SEO and search, small but high-intent), Reforge (needs an original framework).

## Reporter-driven newsletters

Small audiences, high intent, often the best placement now. Examples by area: product and growth, engineering leadership, tech business analysis, tech scoops, tech and policy, Big Tech analysis, tech and culture. B2B SaaS: SaaStr Daily, Demand Curve, Growth Unhinged, Trends.vc. AI: Import AI, The Batch, The Algorithm, Interconnects, AI Tidbits. Read how each author takes pitches (email, direct message or tip box) and follow that.

## Podcasts

A guest spot can beat a news hit: longer attention, replays for years, trust passed on by the host. Examples: SaaS and startups (Lenny's Podcast, My First Million, The Twenty Minute VC, SaaStr Podcast; Acquired and All-In are out of reach for most), AI (No Priors, Latent Space, Practical AI, TWIML AI Podcast, The Cognitive Revolution), developer (The Changelog, Software Engineering Daily, DevTools FM).

How to pitch one:
1. Listen to 3 episodes first.
2. Find the host's preferred channel (direct message, email or guest form).
3. Offer a topic, not yourself: "I could talk about <specific angle>".
4. Bring evidence: other appearances, what is unique, what listeners will learn.
5. Make it easy: a bio, a headshot link and suggested questions.

## Trade and vertical press

Smaller audiences with much higher intent.

| Vertical | Places to look |
|---|---|
| Advertising and marketing | AdAge, Adweek |
| Selling | Modern Sales Pros, Sales Hacker |
| People operations | SHRM, HR Dive, HR Brew |
| Money tech | Finextra, CoinDesk, The Block |
| Health tech | STAT, MobiHealthNews |
| Schools and learning | EdSurge, EdScoop |
| Property | The Real Deal, Inman |
| Law | Law360, Above the Law |
| Climate | Heatmap, Canary Media |
| Engineering tools | DevOps.com, InfoQ |

For another vertical, search the web for "top publications" plus your industry and score the reporters you find as in building-a-media-list.md.

## Regional and local

Worth using if you have a regional story: the home city, a cluster of customers, a public contract. Options: local business journals, public radio affiliates (they welcome a business angle), local TV business slots (wide reach, easier to get), and regional tech news sites.

## Leave out

- Product Hunt, BetaList, Indie Hackers: directories, not press.
- Press-release distribution services: costly for early stage and reporters ignore them. Revisit only when investor-relations or securities rules require them.
- "As featured in" badge sellers: paid fake coverage that hurts.
- Guest-post link networks: search engines penalise them.

## Keep it fresh

Quarterly: confirm tier 1 contacts have not moved. Monthly: add new outlets in your category. Before each pitch: check for a job change. The working copy is `.agents/media-list.md`.
