# Lead magnets

## When to use

Typical wordings: "what could I offer for people's email address", "turn this guide into a freebie", "plan a checklist download", "add a content upgrade to this post", "my opt-in converts badly".

This skill decides what to make, how to gate it, and how to get it seen. Neighbours take over here:
- Interactive calculators, graders and quizzes as the magnet: free-tools.
- Writing the magnet's text: copywriting.
- The email sequence after someone signs up: emails.
- Form and landing page tuning: cro. Popup mechanics: popups.
- Topic selection: content-strategy. Tracking: analytics. Paid push: ad-creative. Social push: social.

## How it works

1. **Load what is already known.** If a product marketing context file exists in the project (look under the agents folder, the claude folder, or an older single-file name), read it and ask only for gaps.
2. **Fill the gaps with questions.** Cover four areas, skipping what is known:
   - Business: what it sells, who buys, which problem it solves.
   - Present capture: current forms, existing magnets, current sign-up rate.
   - Raw material: posts, data, internal templates or expertise that could be repackaged; where the audience hangs out; the question prospects ask most before buying; whether a follow-up sequence exists; budget for design and promotion.
   - Goal: bigger list, better leads, or education; which buyer stage; deadline and capacity.
3. **Apply five tests to every idea.**
   - One narrow problem. "Reply-getting subject lines for cold outreach" beats "an email marketing bundle".
   - Right stage: people early on need teaching, people comparing need an evaluation aid, people ready to buy need help getting set up.
   - Looks worth paying for, yet takes little time to use (a starting point is under 30 minutes, ideally under 10), with something to act on at once.
   - Leads to the product: it solves a problem the product also solves, or reveals a gap the product fills, and shows you know the field.
   - Simple to consume: one format only (never ebook plus video plus sheet), fine on a phone, no special software.
4. **Pick a format.** Relative effort to build (note your own build hours for each, see benchmarks.md):

   | Format | Good for | Effort |
   |---|---|---|
   | Cheat sheet | lookup, shortcuts | low |
   | Checklist | fast results, step-by-step jobs | low |
   | Template (doc, sheet, Notion) | repeatable work | low to medium |
   | Swipe file | examples to copy from | medium |
   | Ebook or guide | depth, authority | high |
   | Email mini-course | teaching plus nurture | medium |
   | Video mini-course | teaching plus personality | high |
   | Quiz or assessment | segmentation, engagement | medium |
   | Webinar | authority, live contact | medium |
   | Resource library | return visits | high |
   | Free trial or community access | product experience | varies |

   Format-by-format build advice is in [formats.md](lead-magnets/formats.md). Stage fit, with sample ideas:
   Then check the pick against the buyer's stage, since the same format can fit one stage and miss another:
   - Still learning about the problem: a "12-point shop-floor safety checklist", a beginner cheat sheet, a broad guide, a "which kind of baker are you" quiz.
   - Weighing options: a supplier comparison sheet, a self-assessment, a bundle of customer stories, a webinar on choosing a tool.
   - Close to buying: reply templates, a trial, a migration checklist, a savings calculator (build it with free-tools).
5. **Decide the gate and the form.**
   - Full gate (everything behind the form): suits valuable, late-funnel content; captures the most addresses but reaches the fewest people.
   - Partial gate (preview open, full version behind the form): a middle path.
   - Ungated with an optional sign-up: suits early teaching content; widest reach, fewest captures.
   - Content upgrade (a bonus tied to one article): relevant to the reader and high intent.

   Fields: email only converts best; add name for personalization; company or role for qualification; long forms only for webinars and demos. Every added field tends to cost some sign-ups, so ask for the least you will really use and compare your own form variants. Frame the swap clearly: state what they get ("the 18-page workbook, free"), show a preview (contents page, first page, sample output), add proof if you have real numbers, and state the unsubscribe promise.

   Consent: before collecting addresses, the form needs a clear opt-in statement, a link to the privacy policy and a working unsubscribe. Use the studio legal skills (start with legal-signoff-orchestrator, and app-legal-privacy-terms-store-review for the policy text); this skill is not legal advice.
6. **Lay out the landing page** in this order: headline stating the benefit; a mockup of the magnet; 3-5 bullets on what is inside; proof; a short form with one clear button; a small FAQ (is it free, what format, who sends it).
7. **Choose delivery.**
   - Instant download: fast, but the address is unverified.
   - Email delivery: verifies the address and opens the relationship, small delay.
   - Thank-you page plus email copy: usually best, instant access and a copy in the inbox.
   - Drip: only for courses and series.

   Use the thank-you page: confirm where the file went, offer one next step (demo, trial, community), offer a ready-made share post, suggest related reading.
8. **Plan distribution.**
   - Blog: inline and end-of-post offers; an upgrade made for one article is more relevant than a generic sidebar box; compare the two on your own pages.
   - Popups: fire on exit intent or scroll depth, and match the offer to the page.
   - Social: teasers, carousels from the key points, the magnet as the profile link.
   - Paid (spend only with the owner's approval and a stated budget): lead ads on social networks for early-stage magnets, search ads for high-intent ones such as templates, professional-network ads for B2B, retargeting for blog readers.
   - Partners: cross-promotion, guest webinars, partner newsletters, resource bundles.
9. **Set measurement.** Track landing page conversion (sign-ups divided by visitors, warm and cold traffic apart), cost per lead, lead-to-customer rate, email engagement (opens and clicks) and time to convert. How to set your own baseline per format, source and audience is in [benchmarks.md](lead-magnets/benchmarks.md). Test one thing at a time: benefit headline vs curiosity headline, format on the same topic, full vs partial gate, email-only vs email plus name, button wording, instant vs emailed delivery. A good magnet shows engagement above your own baseline, leads that reach trial or demo at your own expected rate, few unsubscribes after delivery, and a match with the target customer profile.
10. **Deliver the plan** in five parts: recommendation (format, topic, stage, why, effort); content outline (sections, length, what makes it different); gating and capture plan (what is gated, fields, page structure); distribution plan (channels, upgrades, paid if any); measurement plan (targets, first test).

## Checks before done

- The magnet solves one problem and ends in a path to the product.
- Only the fields that will be used are on the form.
- A consent statement, privacy link and unsubscribe are planned, and the legal skills were pointed to.
- Any paid promotion names a budget and was approved by the owner.
- Targets come from the owner's own baseline, not from outside averages.
- Text is English, with no em or en dashes.
