## When to use

Typical wordings: "how do I show up in AI answers", "get cited by ChatGPT", "optimize for Perplexity", "AI Overviews are eating our clicks", "do we need llms.txt", "what is OKF", "make the site readable for agents", "what is our AI visibility".

Neighbouring skills and where each takes over:
- seo-audit: classic technical and on-page audits. Start there when the problem is crawling, speed or rankings.
- schema: writing and validating the structured data itself. This skill only says which types matter.
- content-strategy, competitors, programmatic-seo, copywriting: planning topics, building comparison pages, pages at scale, and the prose. Hand off once the AI-specific choices are made.

## How it works

1. **Collect context.** If a product marketing context file exists in the repo (check the agents folder, the claude folder, or an older file name), read it first and only ask what it does not answer. Then ask, skipping what is known: the 10-20 queries that matter most; whether AI answers already exist for them and who is cited; content types published (blog, docs, comparisons, product pages); domain strength; whether schema is in place; whether a Wikipedia page or review-site profiles exist; the goal (be cited, appear in AI Overviews, displace a named competitor, fix old pages or write new ones).

2. **Frame the difference.** Classic SEO is about rank. AI SEO is about being picked as a source, and a well-built page that does not rank first can still be cited. Each engine uses its own index (Google AI Overviews: Google's rankings; ChatGPT: wider pool, not just top results; Perplexity: always links sources and favours recent, structured pages; Gemini: Google index plus Knowledge Graph; Copilot: Bing; Claude: reportedly Brave Search when search is on; Anthropic's subprocessor list names Brave Search, but that is reported, not stated in plain words). Per-engine detail is in [platforms](ai-seo/references/platforms.md). This skill quotes no industry statistics. If the user wants numbers (how often AI Overviews appear, click loss, citation shares), find a public study or the vendor's own published figures, name it and its date, or measure it with the monitoring step below.

3. **State Google's position before advising.** Google's guide to its AI features says they are built on the same core ranking and quality systems as normal Search, so ordinary SEO still applies. For Google that means: no special markup, AI text files, llms.txt or markdown are needed; pages do not have to be cut into fragments; writing a second version "for AI", or mass-producing pages for every query variation, counts as scaled content abuse under its spam policy; helpful, reliable, people-first content is the standard (the guide does not use the term E-E-A-T, which belongs to Google's quality rater guidelines). The guide also describes a Generative AI performance report in Search Console. Check the vendor's current documentation for what that report covers before promising anything from it. The other engines (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot) are reported to lift passages that stand alone, such as FAQs, tables and definition blocks, to read llms.txt and to lean on third-party sources. That structure is only tidy writing, so it does no harm for Google. When in doubt, write for people and organize for clarity.

4. **Plan for query fan-out.** Google's AI features run several related searches behind one typed query and merge the results. One page per keyword therefore loses to a page or site that covers the whole topic cluster. For each priority topic, list sibling questions (5-10 is a starting point) the AI is likely to expand into and check that the site answers them. Example: for "best standing desk" the siblings might be height range, motor noise, weight limit, warranty length and cable management.

5. **Audit visibility (read-only).** Run the 10-20 key queries in Google, ChatGPT and Perplexity. Record per query whether an AI answer appears, whether you are cited, and which competitors are. Cover five query shapes, for example for a project-tracking tool: "what is a kanban board", "best task tracker for small agencies", "[brand] vs [rival]", "how to cut meeting time in a remote team", "task tracker pricing per seat". Where a competitor wins and you do not, compare structure, authority signals (stats, quotes), freshness, schema, and third-party presence (Wikipedia, Reddit, review sites).

6. **Check extractability per priority page.** Pass or fail each: definition in the first paragraph; answer blocks that make sense alone; statistics with sources; comparison table for "X vs Y" intent; FAQ in natural-language questions; matching schema (FAQ, HowTo, Article, Product); named author with credentials; updated recently (the last 6 months is a starting point); headings that mirror how people phrase the query; AI bots permitted.

7. **Check bot access.** Read robots.txt for rules against these user agents. OpenAI: OAI-SearchBot (ChatGPT search), GPTBot (model training) and ChatGPT-User (fetches a user asks for; robots rules may not apply to it). Perplexity: PerplexityBot (search results) and Perplexity-User (user-initiated fetches, generally ignores robots.txt). Anthropic: ClaudeBot (training), Claude-SearchBot (search quality) and Claude-User (user-initiated fetches); the older anthropic-ai token is not in Anthropic's current list. Google: Google-Extended only controls use for Gemini training and grounding and does not affect Search or AI Overviews, which follow Googlebot. Microsoft: Bingbot (Copilot). A blocked search bot cannot cite you. Whether to block is the owner's business call, and each OpenAI and Anthropic bot is set separately, so the middle path is to block the training bots (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Google-Extended) and allow the search and user bots. Bot names and behaviour change, so confirm them in each vendor's current documentation. Do not edit robots.txt without the owner's yes. Sample rules are in [platforms](ai-seo/references/platforms.md).

8. **Work in three pillars, in this order of effort:** structure (make it extractable), authority (make it citable), presence (be where AI looks). For a first pass, start with Google AI Overviews, then ChatGPT, then Perplexity; Copilot and Claude matter more for enterprise or developer audiences. Six moves help everywhere: allow the bots, put named-source statistics in the copy, refresh competitive pages monthly, use a clean H1 > H2 > H3 outline, keep pages fast (check the Core Web Vitals thresholds), show author bios with credentials.

9. **Pillar 1, structure.** AI lifts passages, not pages, so each key claim must stand alone. Use a definition block for "what is", numbered steps for "how to", a table for "X vs Y", a pros and cons block for "is it worth it", an FAQ block for common questions, and a sourced-statistic block for numbers. Templates: [patterns](ai-seo/references/patterns.md). Rules: put the answer first; keep answer passages short (40-60 words is a starting point, not a measured value); write headings the way users type queries; prefer tables over prose for comparisons and numbered lists over paragraphs for procedures; one idea per paragraph.

10. **Pillar 2, authority.** A 2024 academic study, Aggarwal et al., "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", KDD 2024, arXiv 2311.09735, of generative engines, tested on a citing search engine, compared several writing tactics. Citing sources, adding statistics and adding expert quotations helped visibility most, fluent writing helped, smaller gains were reported for an expert tone, plainer wording, domain terms and varied vocabulary, and keyword stuffing did not help and scored below the unchanged page on the main measure. Lower-ranked sites gained most from citations. The exact lifts depend on the test setup, so read the study itself before quoting a number. In practice: use specific numbers with a source and a date, prefer original research over summaries of it, quote named people with title and organization, show a visible "last updated" date, refresh competitive pages at least quarterly, remove stale claims, show first-hand experience, and give each article an author with relevant expertise.

11. **Pillar 3, presence.** Third-party mentions often matter as much as your own site: Wikipedia and Reddit show up among the sources assistants cite; trade publications, review sites (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius for B2B software), YouTube and Quora also feed answers. Actions: keep a Wikipedia entry accurate if one exists, join Reddit threads as a real participant, get into industry roundups and comparison articles, keep review profiles current, publish video answers to key how-to queries, answer Quora questions in depth. Never fabricate mentions or spam.

12. **Machine-readable files (optional layer).** Agents increasingly compare products for buyers, and pricing hidden behind scripts or a "contact sales" wall gets skipped. Offer a plain-text pricing file, an llms.txt overview and, if the site qualifies, an OKF bundle. Layouts and rules are in [machine files](ai-seo/references/machine-files.md); OKF details and when to skip it are in [okf](ai-seo/references/okf.md). Say plainly that Google needs none of these.

13. **Schema.** Map content to type: Article or BlogPosting (author, date), HowTo (steps), FAQPage (Q&A), Product (price, reviews), ItemList (comparisons), Review and AggregateRating (trust), Organization (entity). Google's AI optimization guide says structured data is not required for generative AI search and that no special markup is needed; it stays useful for rich results that still exist and as machine-readable facts for other consumers. Schema is advised for non-Google AI engines, but that is reported, not verified. A valid type is not a search appearance: the end of the FAQ rich result in Search on 7 May 2026 is reported (the guide does not mention it), and the how-to rich result was retired earlier, so check Google's current rich result documentation before promising an appearance from FAQPage or HowTo. Hand the implementation to the schema skill.

14. **Agent-friendly pages.** Autonomous agents read a page by rendering it, by parsing the DOM, or through the accessibility tree. So: render real content without a pile of scripts, use semantic elements (main, nav, article, button, one heading outline, image alt text), label every control, avoid layouts that rebuild on each click, and keep pricing, specs and contact details on a public indexable page. Google also mentions a forthcoming commerce protocol (UCP) for agents; watch it, nothing to build yet. Ecom and local sites: keep Merchant Center feeds and the Business Profile current.

15. **Pick the right content type.** Types that are often cited: comparison articles, definitive guides, original research, best-of lists, product pages, opinion and analysis, how-to guides. Which type wins differs by query, so check which types the AI answers cite for your own queries in the visibility audit. Weak performers: unstructured generic posts, thin marketing-fluff product pages, gated content, undated or unsigned pages, PDF-only material. Per-format advice: [content types](ai-seo/references/content-types.md).

16. **Monitor monthly.** Track: whether AI Overviews appear for your queries, brand citation rate, share of AI voice versus competitors, how the AI describes you, and which of your pages are cited (via referral traffic from AI sources in GA4). Paid trackers exist (Otterly AI, Peec AI, ZipTie, LLMrefs); Semrush and Ahrefs also report AI Overview data and Search Console covers normal Google performance. Free route: every month run the top 20 queries through ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google, log who is cited and which page in a sheet, compare to last month. Search Console covers Google only, so cross-engine results come from these checks or paid tools. Name the cost of any paid tool and ask before signing up.

## Checks before done

- Google advice stayed inside its stated position; nothing was recommended as a "special AI file" for Google, and no search appearance was promised from FAQ or HowTo markup.
- No separate AI-only copy, no content chopped into fragments, no mass thin pages, no invented mentions or citations.
- Every statistic in the deliverable has a named source and date.
- Search bots are not blocked unless the owner chose it; no robots.txt or live-site change was made without a yes.
- Main content is not gated, script-only, or PDF-only.
- Pages carry a visible updated date and a named author.
- Common failure list reviewed: ignoring AI search, treating it as separate from SEO, writing for machines, undated pages, gated content, no third-party presence, no schema, keyword stuffing, hidden pricing, blocked bots, vague claims with no data, no monitoring.
- A monthly re-check is scheduled.
