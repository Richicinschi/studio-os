# Growth and scaling models

- **Feedback loops.** An output that feeds the next input either accelerates growth or accelerates decline. Draw the loop on paper: more readers, more shares, more readers. Strengthen the link that is weakest, and watch for negative loops such as slow support leading to bad reviews.
- **Compounding.** Small steady gains stack up. Weekly publishing, a growing email list and steady reputation work look slow for months, then pull ahead. Start early and keep the rhythm.
- **Network effects.** Some products improve as more people join: shared boards, integrations, marketplaces, user communities. If yours does, design the first-use experience around inviting the second person.
- **Flywheel.** Effort that is hard at first eventually keeps itself turning: guides bring visitors, visitors become customers, customers supply case studies, case studies feed better guides. Name each link so you can find the stuck one.
- **Switching costs.** What it costs a customer in time, money, data and learning to leave. Raise them by genuine value only: deeper integrations, stored history, tailored workflows. Never use hostage tactics such as blocking export of their own data.
- **Exploration vs exploitation.** Keep tuning what works, and set aside a fixed slice (for instance one month in six, or a tenth of budget; a starting point, not a measured value) to test new ideas. Both starve if either is dropped.
- **Critical mass (tipping point).** Self-sustaining growth begins past a threshold. Win one narrow segment completely before widening: depth first, breadth second.
- **Survivorship bias.** We see the winners and not the many failures behind them. Study campaigns that failed as well as those that worked before copying a famous success, and ask what the winners had that cannot be copied.
