# Paywall experiment bank

Pick one variable per test. Groups follow the order of a paywall journey.

## Contents
- Timing and trigger
- Layout and presentation
- Price display and plans
- Words
- Trial design
- Personalisation
- Frequency and dismissal

## Timing and trigger

- Prompt right after the first win versus when the user taps a locked item.
- Trial reminder a week out versus the day before.
- Prompt after a number of actions versus after a number of days.
- Prompt level tied to engagement (light user, heavy user).
- Hard block versus soft gate with a preview.
- Locked feature versus usage cap as the main trigger.
- In-context sheet versus a full upgrade page.
- Small banner versus modal.
- A reminder shown on free-plan pages when the user is about to leave.

## Layout and presentation

- Full screen versus overlay.
- Lean, button-first page versus a feature-rich one.
- One plan shown versus a side-by-side table.
- With a screenshot or preview versus text only.
- Benefit sentences versus a bullet list of features.
- What the user gains versus what they would lose.
- Summary built from the user's own usage.
- Short demo clip or animated preview versus a static image.
- A recap of what the user has already made.
- A simple savings calculator, only with numbers you can defend.

## Price display and plans

- Monthly, annual, or both with a switch.
- Savings as an amount versus a percentage.
- Per-day framing versus per-month.
- Price shown first versus after the user taps in.
- One recommended plan versus several tiers; two versus three visible.
- A "best value" tag on one plan (only if true).
- A one-time purchase beside the subscription.
- Showing a custom or business tier versus hiding it.
- A referral reward as a price incentive.
- Intro discount for the first period; loyalty price after long free use; annual bundle.
- Time-limited offers only when the deadline is real and not reset.

## Words

- Benefit headline versus feature headline.
- Question versus statement.
- Value-led versus loss-led (keep it free of guilt).
- Headline using the user's own data.
- A headline carrying real social proof, such as a true user count.
- CTA wording: start trial, continue, upgrade, a benefit-specific verb.
- "My" versus "your" in the button.
- Price inside the button versus beside it.
- Reassurance lines: cancel anytime, refund terms, a short FAQ, a support link. Only state what is true.

## Trial design

- Length: 7, 14 or 30 days.
- Card up front versus none.
- Full access versus limited access.
- Extension for engaged users; a second trial for lapsed ones.
- Countdown always visible versus only near the end.
- Reminder email count and timing.
- Grace period versus instant downgrade.
- Pause option versus plain cancel.
- One-tap purchase from the paywall versus separate checkout; stored payment details for returning users.
- Several payment methods; quarterly plan next to monthly and annual.
- Invite flow for a solo user growing into a team.

## Personalisation

- Copy based on features used; lead with the most-used paid feature.
- Usage counters in the headline.
- Plan recommended from behaviour.
- Different paywalls for heavy and casual users.
- Business versus consumer wording; role or industry wording.
- Wording matched to the traffic source.

## Frequency and dismissal

- Prompts per session; cool-down of hours versus days.
- Rising urgency versus steady tone (rising false urgency is a dark pattern, avoid).
- One prompt per feature versus a merged one.
- Re-show rules after a big engagement jump.
- Dismiss wording: later, no thanks, remind me tomorrow.
- Asking why they declined, offering a lower tier, a short exit survey.
- Warm versus neutral decline copy.
