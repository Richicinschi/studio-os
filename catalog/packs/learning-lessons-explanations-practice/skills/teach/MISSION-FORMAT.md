# MISSION.md format

`MISSION.md` sits at the top of the teaching workspace and states why the learner is doing this. When you wonder what to teach, which source to bring or which exercise to build, the answer should lead back to this file.

## Template

```md
# Mission: {Topic}

## Why
{One to three sentences naming the real goal. What will be different in their life or work once they can do this? Push past vague aims like "understand X" to the result underneath.}

## Success looks like
- {Something specific you could watch them do}
- {Another one}

## Constraints
- {Time, money, existing duties, preferred ways to learn: anything that limits the plan}

## Out of scope
- {Neighbouring topics they have chosen not to chase for now; this keeps the next lesson close to their edge}
```

## Rules

- **One mission per workspace.** Two unrelated subjects mean two workspaces.
- **Specific beats general.** "Order lunch in Portuguese on my May trip" is better than "get good at languages"; "build a budget spreadsheet my partner can use" is better than "learn Excel".
- **Question vague answers.** If they cannot say why, interview them first and write nothing. A weak mission does more harm than none.
- **Update it when the goal moves.** A stale mission quietly steers later sessions the wrong way.
- **Keep it brief.** More than one screen means it has turned into a plan.
