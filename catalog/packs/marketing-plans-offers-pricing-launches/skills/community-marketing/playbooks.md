# Community playbooks

## Launching from zero

The counts in this playbook are starting points, not measured values.

1. Invite the first 20 to 50 members yourself: your most active users, beta testers, fans. Keep the doors closed until there is steady baseline chatter.
2. Write guidelines that describe the feel of good participation (what a great post looks like here), then the rules.
3. Before opening, put 5 to 10 example posts in the channels: a question, a small win, a useful resource. They model the behaviour you want.
4. Do the unscalable work early: answer every post, greet each newcomer by name, host a weekly call. This is what creates the first impression of a living place.
5. Choose one action members should take every week, make it quick and easy, and reward it where everyone can see.

## Growing an existing community

1. Find where people fall away. Are they joining and never posting? Posting once and vanishing? Fix the leakiest stage first.
2. Build a newcomer path: pinned welcome post, an introductions channel, a personal message or email from the community manager, and a clear "start here" route.
3. Show off member results in public: projects, testimonials, milestones. It tells everyone that taking part pays off.
4. Add recurring rituals: a weekly "what are you shipping" thread, a monthly AMA, a seasonal challenge. Repetition builds habit.
5. Back the power users. In most communities a small share of members produces most of the value; find yours in your own activity data and give them recognition, early access, moderator roles or a voice in product decisions.

## Ambassador or advocate program

1. Start with people who already recommend you without being asked: check reviews, social mentions, community posts.
2. Reach out one to one and say why they in particular. A generic form signals that they are interchangeable.
3. Offer real benefits: exclusive access, swag, a revenue share, or public recognition. "Early access to features" alone is thin.
4. Equip them: referral links, shareable assets, talking points, a private channel.
5. Track referral traffic, signups and engagement per ambassador, keep what works, cut what does not.
6. Studio rule: ambassadors disclose their relationship when they promote, never post from fake or duplicate accounts, and follow each platform's rules on promotion. Outreach goes out only with the owner's word.

## Community-led support

1. Turn the most-asked community questions into a searchable knowledge base.
2. Recognise people who help others: expert badges, leaderboards, shoutouts.
3. Close the loop with the product team. When a member's feedback causes a change, say so publicly and credit them.
4. Read sentiment weekly and watch for repeated complaints or confusion; those are early churn signals.
