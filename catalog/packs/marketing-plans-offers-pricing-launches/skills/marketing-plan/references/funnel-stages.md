# The five-stage funnel (AARRR) and how to sequence by it

| Stage | Question it answers | Typical measures |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | How do strangers learn we exist? | visits, leads, signup-page sessions, store page views, CAC by channel |
| Activation | After trying us, do they reach a moment that converts? | signup completion, time to first value, first key action, trial to paid |
| Retention | Do they stay and use more? | daily/weekly/monthly actives, week 1, 4 and 12 retention, churn |
| Referral | Do kept customers bring others? | viral coefficient, NPS, attributed referrals |
| Revenue | Who pays, how much, how does it grow? | revenue per user, LTV, expansion, ARR or MRR |

Boundary rule, applied everywhere: a visitor reaching the signup page is acquisition; finishing signup and everything after (first key action, trial to paid) is activation.

## Why tag every move by stage

- It turns a channel list into a priority question: which stage is the bottleneck now?
- A leak further down usually costs more than missing traffic, so fix it before adding water.
- Revenue and referral get an honest hearing rather than hiding under "growth".

Brand and content are not a sixth stage. Voice governs every stage's copy; content feeds acquisition (search, social), activation (onboarding copy), retention (lifecycle), referral (talking points) and revenue (pricing pages, sales material). They show up in the strategic frame and the cross-cutting rows of the stack.

## Finding the bottleneck

Match what the numbers show to the stage that is binding:

| Symptom | Binding stage | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Few or no users | Acquisition | pre-launch, waitlist, first 100 users |
| Visitors arrive, then leave | Activation | low signup completion, weak store conversion, broken onboarding, or a day-1 paid rate far below the day-30 rate (the product converts with time but the first session does not bridge the gap) |
| Activated users vanish | Retention | month-1 retention below comparable products or your own earlier cohorts, a sharp early drop (read the day from your cohort curve), short LTV |
| Users stay but growth is slow | Referral and revenue | fans do not share, referrals arrive unstructured, prices untested, revenue per user low for the value |
| Every stage healthy at small scale | Acquisition again | the task is now scaling |

## Per-stage habits

**Acquisition.** Ask where awareness trails the market and which channels competitors have crowded. Moves: search content, founder-led social, listing optimisation, press, events, partnerships (newsletter swaps, integrations, resellers), commerce surfaces, B2B sales support, paid later. Build organic first; paid on a broken base only amplifies the break.

**Activation.** Ask where in the first session the user decides yes or no. Moves: fix broken gates, onboarding rebuild (often the highest-leverage single move), listing rewrite, lifecycle email timing, paywall and trial length, free-to-paid bridges. Cut anything between opening and first felt value.

**Retention.** Ask why people leave and what the second moment of value is. Moves: lifecycle flows, preference centres, aligning churn definitions, hardware-to-software paths, annual defaults, support as marketing, community. Ship flows in order of content stability: post-purchase for physical goods first, onboarding emails last (they cite screens that change), win-back as a recurring campaign.

**Referral.** Ask what inbound interest is going uncaptured and what the natural share moment is. Moves: ambassador or affiliate program, in-product share prompts, founder as referrer zero, expert networks, gifting, two-sided rewards. Launch with the handful already volunteering; do not wait for a complete program.

**Revenue.** Ask whether the company underprices, underpackages or misses an upsell. Moves: pricing audit, annual default, bundles, shop page work, B2B case studies, long-term value pools flagged only. Audit before experimenting; discounts and plan mix often make dashboard price differ from list price.

## Assigning a move to one stage

Put it where its main measurable effect lands, and note crossovers. Example: a store-listing rewrite helps discovery and trial; the measured effect is trial conversion, so it lives under activation. If unsure, ask where removing the move would hurt most.

## Reading the mix

Uneven sections are information: long acquisition means traffic is the bottleneck; long activation means conversion is broken (common in beta); long retention means churn after scaling; long referral means loyalty without sharing mechanics; long revenue means underpricing or missing layers. A plan spread evenly across five stages usually means the diagnosis was weak; revisit the funnel intake.

## Order of presentation

Always walk the funnel in order: acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue. Show priority through the executive summary and through depth, not through reordering.
