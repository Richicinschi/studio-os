# Sequence templates

Four ready outlines. Day numbers count from the trigger. These are starting points, not measured values. Shift them to fit the audience and the sales cycle.

## Welcome (after signup), 5-7 emails over about two weeks

The outline lists seven; drop the weakest for a shorter cycle.

Goal: activate, build trust, convert.

1. Immediately. Subject idea: "You are in: start with [Product] today". Hand over what was promised, name one next action, say how often you will write.
2. Day 2-3. "Reach your first [result] before lunch". A small, quick win that builds confidence, with a helpful link.
3. Day 4-5. "The problem behind [Product]". The origin or mission, showing you understand their problem.
4. Day 6-7. "What [Customer] changed to reach [Result]". A case study or testimonial close to their situation, ending on a soft invitation to look around.
5. Day 8-9. An objection headline such as "Too busy to set up X?". Name the usual hesitation, reframe it, show the short path.
6. Day 10-12. "Still haven't opened [Feature]?". Spotlight an underused capability and its benefit, with a direct link to try it.
7. Day 13-15. "Time to [upgrade/buy/commit]?". Recap the value, make a clear offer, add urgency only if it is real, and reduce risk with a guarantee or trial.

## Lead nurture (before a sale), 6-8 emails over 2-3 weeks

The outline lists eight; trim for a short sales cycle.

Goal: build trust, show expertise, convert.

1. Immediately. Deliver the lead magnet, introduce yourself briefly, preview what follows.
2. Day 3-4. An insight that extends the lead magnet; shows expertise; light link to more content.
3. Day 5-6. Describe their problem in depth so they feel understood; hint that a fix exists.
4. Day 7-9. Your method or framework, taught rather than sold, leading toward the product.
5. Day 10-12. A concrete customer result, specific and relatable, with a soft call to action.
6. Day 13-15. What makes your approach different; deal fairly with the alternatives.
7. Day 16-19. Answer the most common concern through an FAQ or myth-busting, to lower friction.
8. Day 20-22. The direct offer: clear pitch, strong value, one specific action, urgency if genuine.

## Re-engagement, 3-4 emails over about 2 weeks

Start after a long quiet spell (roughly one to two months without activity, tune it to your product). Goal: win the person back or clean the list.

1. Check-in. "Did we lose you, [Name]?" Show real concern, ask what happened, offer one easy way back.
2. 3-4 days later. "You once [achieved X], here is what is new". Recall past value, mention what is new, short call to action.
3. 6-8 days later. "A reason to come back, [Name]". A time-limited incentive, only if it fits.
4. 11-15 days later. A blunt subject asking whether to stop writing to them. Be candid, and give a single click to remain or leave. Remove non-responders from the list.

## Product onboarding, 5-7 emails over about two weeks

Goal: activate, reach the first moment of value, then upgrade.

Coordinate with the in-app onboarding: email supports it and never duplicates it.

1. Immediately. Confirm the signup, point to the single critical action, link straight to it.
2. Day 2. Sent only to people who have not done that action: a tip or short video and a way to get help.
3. Day 3-4. One key feature, a specific use case, an in-app link.
4. Day 5-6. A customer who succeeded, with a journey the reader can relate to.
5. Day 8. "How is it going?" Ask for feedback and offer help.
6. Day 11-13. A power feature for engaged users, a level-up lesson.
7. Day 15 and after. Trial users get the conversion push, free users an upgrade prompt, paid users an expansion suggestion.
