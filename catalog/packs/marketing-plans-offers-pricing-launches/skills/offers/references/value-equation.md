# Value equation: finding the weak lever

A buyer weighs what they expect to get against what they pay. Four things set the "what they expect to get":

```
          Outcome they want  x  Belief it will happen to them
Value  =  ------------------------------------------------------
          Wait until the result  x  Work and risk they carry
```

Price is only the thing value is compared with. If value feels lower than price, no wording on the page rescues the sale. So a request to cut the price is usually a request to lift the top line or shrink the bottom one.

## Lever 1: the outcome they want (top)

Buyers state a surface ask. The outcome sits one or two levels behind it, and it is often about identity or relief.

| Surface ask | Outcome behind it |
|---|---|
| "Build me a booking page" | "Fewer empty slots in my diary each week" |
| "Teach me bookkeeping" | "Close the quarter without a weekend of panic" |
| "A workout plan" | "Walk up the stairs at work without thinking about my knee" |
| "A budget spreadsheet" | "Stop dreading the credit card statement" |
| "Cheaper hosting" | "A site I never have to think about during a holiday" |

To lift this lever: name the outcome in the buyer's own words, tie it to the larger goal, and describe an ordinary Tuesday after it has worked. The common mistake is pitching the deliverable ("a booking page") when the buyer is paying for the change in their week.

## Lever 2: belief it will happen to them (top)

Many offers have a fine outcome and still stall because the buyer thinks "it works for others, not for me". This lever is the one most often ignored.

To lift it:
- Show real proof: named customers with permission, real numbers, before and after. Only proof you actually have. If you have none, say the offer is new and lean on a small paid pilot or a narrow guarantee instead.
- Name your method so it can be repeated and discussed.
- Show people who resemble the buyer.
- Add a guarantee that matches the real fear (see guarantees.md).
- Say what can go wrong and how you handle it.

Common mistake: adding more features when the buyer needs more proof. Features serve the outcome lever. Proof serves this one.

## Lever 3: wait until the result (bottom)

Slow results hurt twice: they feel slow, and they erode belief.

To shrink it:
- Engineer a small first win inside the first week.
- Replace long kickoff meetings with a short written intake.
- Hand over templates and files on day one, not drip them.
- Give a plain "do this first today" step.
- Shorten the full timeline only if you can really hit it.

Common mistake: promising a speed you cannot keep. One public miss damages every later buyer's belief.

## Lever 4: work and risk they carry (bottom)

Everything the buyer pays besides money: effort, learning, decisions, willpower, fear of looking foolish, what they give up while doing this.

To shrink it:
- Offer a done-for-you tier and price it accordingly.
- Choose defaults and recommend, so the buyer decides less.
- Supply examples and templates so the learning curve is short.
- Name the emotional, social and opportunity risks and answer each.
- Prefer asynchronous delivery for busy buyers.
- Use reminders or accountability so willpower is not the plan.

Common mistake: assuming the buyer enjoys the process. They want the result. The exception is identity buyers (fitness, craft, mastery) for whom doing the work is part of the outcome.

## Scoring a stuck offer

Score each lever from 1 to 10, honestly, and write one sentence of evidence per score.

- Outcome: can the buyer repeat the result back to a friend in one line?
- Belief: are there at least three comparable proof points, a repeatable method, and an answer to "my case is different"?
- Wait: what is the first win, when does it arrive, where does time leak (onboarding lag, drip content, week-one friction)?
- Work and risk: how many hours, decisions, live sessions, and fears does the buyer carry?

The lowest score is the constraint. If two tie, take the one that is cheaper to move and unlocks the other.

### Worked example: a stalled photography workshop

Scores before: outcome 7 ("take better portraits"), belief 3 (one review, method unnamed), wait 6 (ten weeks, no early win), work 5 (weekly live critiques).

Belief is the lowest, so work there first: name the method as a four-step shoot routine, publish six before and after sets from past students with their consent, and add a beginners track with named graduates. Re-score belief. Only then turn to work and risk, for instance by turning the weekly live critique into a recorded review plus one live session a month.

## The test that tells you it is working

The buyer can say the outcome back in their own words, and does not ask "will this really work?" If either question keeps coming up in sales calls or replies, the problem is the offer, not the copy.
