# Brand voice framework

## Voice and tone

**Voice** is the brand's personality, and it stays put. **Tone** is how that personality bends to the moment, and it moves.

A warm brand might cheer loudly when a customer reaches a milestone and speak softly when answering a complaint. Same voice, two tones.

## Building a voice

### 1. Choose three to five traits

Pair each with what it must not tip into:
- Confident, not arrogant
- Friendly, not sloppy
- Knowledgeable, not talking down
- Inventive, not gimmicky
- Genuine, not slangy

### 2. Draw the voice chart

| Trait | Meaning | Do | Don't |

| [Trait] | [What it means here] | [Sample line] | [Sample line] |

### 3. Adapt to the setting

| Setting | Shift | Sample |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Social media | Looser | "Quick one for you today" |
| Support | Warmer, more understanding | "That sounds frustrating, let's sort it" |
| Legal | More formal | "Pursuant to the terms below" |
| Sales | Surer | "Most teams see a change in week one" |

## Four tests

Hold a draft up and ask:
1. Does it sound like us?
2. Could a competitor have written it?
3. Will our audience connect with it?
4. Does it match what we stand for?
