# Content block patterns for answer engines

Fill-in shapes. Replace every bracket with real content from the page's own subject; never paste a template unfilled.

## Answer-engine blocks (snippets, overviews, voice)

**Definition** (for "what is X"): heading "What is [term]?", then one sentence defining it, one or two sentences on its key traits, one on why it matters.

**Steps** (for "how to X"): heading, one-sentence overview, a numbered list where each item is a bold step name plus one or two sentences of action, and an optional closing line with the expected result or time.

**Comparison table** (for "X vs Y"): heading naming both options, a table with one row per criterion and a final "best for" row, then a "bottom line" sentence recommending by need.

**Pros and cons** (for "is X worth it"): one-sentence context, a Pros list and a Cons list with bold category labels, then a verdict of one or two balanced sentences.

**FAQ** (for pages with several common questions; also the base for FAQ schema): each question as a heading, worded as people search, answer begins with the direct reply and adds two or three supporting sentences. Use natural phrasing ("How do I" rather than "How does one"), include the question words what, how, why, when, where, who, which, match the "People also ask" box, and keep each answer short (50-100 words is a starting point, not a measured value).

**Listicle** (for "best X", "top X", "N ways to X"): heading with the count, a short intro that states the selection criteria, then a numbered sub-heading per item with two or three sentences on why it earned a place.

## Citation blocks (assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini)

**Sourced statistic**: claim, then "according to [organization]" and the number with its timeframe, then why it matters. Statistics with sources give an engine something it can cite.

**Expert quote**: the quote in quotation marks, then the person's name, title and organization, then one sentence of interpretation. Only use real quotes you can verify; never invent a speaker.

**Authoritative claim**: topic plus a specific verb and claim, a named source that confirms it with the evidence, then the implication in plain words.

**Self-contained answer**: a bold label (the topic or question) followed by two or three sentences that make full sense with no surrounding text, with a number or example inside.

**Evidence sandwich**: opening claim, a short bullet list of two or three sourced data points, closing sentence that turns the evidence into an action.

## Domain adjustments

- Technology: exact terminology, version numbers and dates, links to official docs, code samples where useful.
- Health: peer-reviewed studies with publication details, credentialed reviewers, study limits noted, a "last reviewed" date.
- Finance: name the regulator, give figures with timeframes, say it is education and not advice, cite recognized institutions.
- Legal: cite statutes and regulations, state the jurisdiction, include a disclaimer, say when a professional is needed.
- Business and marketing: case studies with measured results, industry reports, percentage changes with dates, quotes from recognized figures.

## Voice search

Spoken queries arrive as whole questions, typically starting with what, how, where, why, when or who. Open with the direct answer in one short sentence (under 30 words is a starting point), use everyday speech, skip jargon unless the audience is expert, add local context where relevant, and shape the answer so it works as one spoken reply.
