# GLOSSARY.md format

`GLOSSARY.md` fixes the vocabulary of the teaching workspace. Lessons, exercises and learning records must all use its words. Writing an entry is also a test: if the learner can squeeze an idea into a sharp definition, they understand it.

## Structure

```md
# {Topic} Glossary

{One or two sentences on what this glossary covers.}

## Terms

**Cadence**:
Steps taken per minute while running.
_Avoid_: Turnover, step rate

**Tempo run**:
A sustained effort at a pace that feels comfortably hard, held for a fixed stretch of the session.
_Avoid_: Threshold jog, hard run

**RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)**:
A self-score from 1 to 10 of how tough an effort felt, with 10 meaning nothing more could be given.
_Avoid_: Effort number, difficulty score
```

## Rules

- **Add a term only once the learner can use it.** This file records ideas they have already compressed; it is not a dictionary to learn from. A concept just introduced waits until the learner uses it correctly.
- **Choose one word.** If several names exist for one idea, pick the best and list the others as words to avoid. Choosing is how a vocabulary gets smaller.
- **Keep it tight.** Two sentences at most, saying what the thing is rather than what it does or how to do it.
- **Reuse your own terms.** Once a word is defined here, use it everywhere, even inside other definitions. That makes hard terms easier later.
- **Use headings for clusters** (say `## Anatomy` or `## Planning`) when groups appear. A single flat list is fine when the terms belong together.
- **Call out ambiguity.** If the wider field uses a word loosely, state the meaning in this workspace: "Here, 'run' always means a continuous effort, and walking breaks are logged separately."
- **Edit as understanding grows.** A first-week definition may be wrong by week six. Fix it where it stands and leave no outdated entries.
