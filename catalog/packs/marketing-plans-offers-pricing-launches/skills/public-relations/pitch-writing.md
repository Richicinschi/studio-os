# Writing a pitch

Rules for pitches to reporters, in the order you need them. Everything here produces drafts for review; the owner decides what is sent.

## Length and shape

A pitch is at most 150 words (the skill's own guideline). If it will not fit, the story is not clear yet. Use five parts:

1. One line on why you are writing to this person (a piece of theirs, their beat).
2. The story in one sentence.
3. Why their readers care, not why you do.
4. Proof: a figure, a customer, a quote, a link to a one-page source.
5. The ask: an interview, an embargo, a quote or a link.

## Voice

- Sound like a person, not a release.
- Refer to something they actually wrote.
- No emoji unless they use them.
- No pleasantry opener and no "did you see my email".

Hype words to keep out: synergy, leverage, seamless, robust, holistic, disruptive, revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, best-in-class, paradigm shift. Also "AI-powered", unless that truly is what sets you apart. Filler idioms such as "at the end of the day" go too.

## Subject lines

- Short enough that a phone does not cut them (under 50 characters is a starting point).
- Lead with the specific detail: a number beats a topic label.
- Promise a story, not a product.
- Prefixes that signal value: "Exclusive:", "Data:", "Op-ed pitch:", "Re: <their headline>".
- Test: would you open it in an inbox of 200?

Patterns that work: "<number> of <group> do <surprising thing>", "Exclusive: <milestone>", "Re: <their headline>", a sharp thesis. Patterns that get deleted: "Press release: ...", "<Company> announces ...", "Story idea for you", "Following up", and any hype adjective.

## Six shapes

Pick the one that matches the story. Fill every bracket from real material.

**Data story.** Subject: the finding and what follows from it. Body: you noticed their recent piece; you analysed or surveyed N items; three bullets with the figures; one sentence on the pattern; a link to a one-page method note (not the home page); an offer of raw data, a call or a relevant customer.

**Exclusive milestone.** Subject starts with "Exclusive:". Body: the story, why it matters to their readers, what is actually new; the embargo time with day, hour and timezone; a link to the kit; two call slots.

**Op-ed.** Subject: "Op-ed pitch:" and the thesis. Body: a compliment on a specific point of theirs; a proposed length of about 700 words and a headline; three argument points, the last the surprising one; why you; why now; a delivery date; willingness to match house style.

**Customer story.** Subject names their beat and the outcome. Body: the customer has agreed to speak on the record (confirm this before drafting); the hook is a specific action and a measurable result; the odd detail; name, title, company; availability; what they will share; you handle the introduction.

**Trend connector.** Subject: a trend and the number of signals. Body: three linked signals, the third being yours and brief; the pattern in one sentence; an admission it may be early; an offer of data or other voices.

**Newsjack reply.** Subject: "Re:" and their headline plus "quick data point". Body: you saw the piece; one sentence of insight; its source and method in a line each; one quotable sentence you would accept in print; a phone number for the day.

## Embargoes and exclusives

- Offer an embargo only to reporters you know or who are known to keep them. State day, time and timezone. A broken embargo ends the relationship.
- An exclusive is a strong lever: use it rarely, only for a first-tier outlet, be honest about its scope (for example one outlet in one country), and have a plan for what you publish the next day.

## Follow-up

- Day 0: the pitch. (The follow-up days below are a starting cadence, not measured values.)
- Day 3: one follow-up, only if you have something new.
- Day 7: a last note with a fresh hook.
- After that, stop until there is genuinely new news.

Never bump a thread, repeat it silently, or send the same pitch reformatted.

## What kills a pitch

- Wrong name or wrong outlet.
- A topic they say they do not cover.
- A release attached as a PDF; paste the key lines instead.
- A long signature with logos and legal text.
- Copying five other reporters.
- A tone of "as per my last email".
- Asking for an NDA before talking.
- A story you cannot back: no customer who will talk, no data ready.

## Track results

Keep a sheet with date sent, subject, reporter, outlet, response (open, reply, pass, coverage) and what they said. After a few dozen pitches (a starting point) the patterns of subject and angle that work for you show up.
