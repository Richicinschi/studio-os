# Page sections, templates and writing tips

Counts such as "3 to 5 benefits" or "5 to 10 objections" are the skill's own starting points, not measured values.

## Core sections
- **Hero:** headline, subheadline, main button, a visual (product screenshot), optionally a proof strip.
- **Proof strip:** recognisable customer logos rather than many; one headline metric; a rating with its review count; a short quote. All must be real.
- **Problem:** describe the problem better than the reader can, so they think "that is me", and hint at what ignoring it costs.
- **Benefits:** bridge from problem to solution with 3 to 5 benefits, never ten. Each gets a headline, a short explanation, and proof if there is any.
- **How it works:** 3 to 4 numbered steps, each an action plus its result.
- **Final call:** recap the value, repeat the button, reverse the risk (trial terms, guarantee, cancel anytime), only if those terms are true.

## Supporting sections
- Testimonials: full quote, name, role, company, photo if possible, a concrete result over praise. Formats: cards, video, embedded posts.
- Case studies: problem, solution, results with specifics; teasers can link out.
- Use cases: let visitors recognise themselves ("for teams who need X").
- Built-for blocks: say plainly who it is for.
- FAQ: 5 to 10 real objections; helps search and support load.
- Comparison: against rivals (named or not) or against the status quo such as spreadsheets; table or side by side.
- Integrations: logos and a line about fitting their stack.
- Founder story: why you built it, what you believe; sets you apart from faceless rivals.
- Demo or tour: interactive demo, video, short animated previews.
- Pricing teaser: a starting price on non-pricing pages.
- Guarantee: refund, trial terms, cancellation.
- Stats: customer count, rating, savings. Only measured numbers.

## Templates
- **Weak: a feature list.** Hero, four features in a row, button. It lists; it does not persuade.
- **Strong: a varied narrative.** Hero, proof strip, problem, how it works, two or three benefits, testimonial, use cases or personas, comparison, case study snippet, FAQ, final call with guarantee.
- **Compact (for ad traffic):** hero, proof strip, three benefits, testimonial, three steps, final call.
- **Enterprise or B2B:** outcome headline, logo bar, business pain, solution overview, use cases by role, security and compliance, integrations, case study with metrics, return-on-investment section, demo or contact button.
- **Launch:** hero with the announcement, demo video, 3 to 5 highlights, before and after, early reactions, launch offer, button with a genuine deadline if one exists.

## Writing tips
- Problem openers: "Sound familiar?", "As a {role}, you probably...", "Most days, {audience} lose hours to...". Then give the specific frustration, the time or money lost, and the knock-on effect.
- Benefit block: headline = the result; body = how, in one or two sentences; proof = number, quote or example (optional).
- Steps: numbered, one simple verb each (connect, set up, get), each showing what you get. Example: link your shop in one minute; pick the products to track; receive a stock alert each morning.
- Choosing testimonials: keep ones with a concrete result, a before and after, a named role and company, and a quotable line. Skip "Five stars", "Highly recommend" and "Works fine".
