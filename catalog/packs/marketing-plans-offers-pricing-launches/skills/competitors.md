# Competitor and alternative pages

## When to use

Typical wordings: "write an alternative page for X", "make a vs page", "build a comparison page", "Acme vs Zed", "how do we compare to X", "battle card", "competitor teardown", "competitive landing pages". Use it for any page that positions the product against a rival, whether the goal is search traffic, helping evaluators, or winning switchers.

Four formats are covered: one rival's alternative page (singular), a roundup of alternatives (plural), you versus one rival, and rival versus rival.

Neighbours and where each takes over:
- Pure sales material (decks, objection sheets, internal battle cards for reps): the sales-enablement skill.
- Researching one rival in depth with no page to publish: the competitor-profiling skill in this pack.
- Page copy polish, search audits, structured data, pages at scale: the marketing-copy-emails-seo-signups pack.

## How it works

1. **Gather context.** If the project has a product marketing context file, read it first and ask only for what is missing. Otherwise collect: the product's core promise, differentiators, ideal customer, pricing model, and its honest strengths and weaknesses; the direct and adjacent rivals, how each is positioned, and search demand for each rival's name; the goal (search traffic, sales help, winning switchers, brand position). Also ask: why do customers switch to you, do you have switcher quotes, how does your price compare, do you offer migration help.
2. **Hold four principles.**
   - Be honest. Name what the rival does well and where you fall short. Readers cross-check every claim.
   - Go deep. Explain why a difference matters and for whom, with a scenario, not a bare feature list.
   - Help the reader decide. Say who each product suits so the evaluation gets shorter.
   - Keep one source of truth. Rival facts live in one record each, and pages read from it.
3. **Pick the format** and follow its outline in [page-formats.md](competitors/references/page-formats.md): singular alternative (reader wants to leave a named rival), plural alternatives (reader is still browsing, give 4 to 7 real options, the skill's own guideline), you vs rival (reader is comparing you directly), rival vs rival (reader compares two others, you appear as the third option).
4. **Build the records.** One file per rival, plus one for your own product written with the same candour. Fields and a worked sample are in [data-record.md](competitors/references/data-record.md). Pages are assembled from these records, so a price change is made once.
5. **Write the sections.** Every page opens with a short summary for skimmers, then uses paragraphs and not only tables. Cover features (how each side handles it, strengths, limits, a bottom line), pricing (tiers, what each includes, hidden costs, yearly cost for a sample team size), who each option suits, and migration (what moves, what must be redone, help offered, switcher quotes). Reusable patterns are in [section-patterns.md](competitors/references/section-patterns.md).
6. **Research each rival from public sources only.** Use the product through its normal public signup or trial if its terms allow, record features and limits, read current public pricing, mine public review sites (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius) for repeated praise and complaints, talk to customers who moved in either direction, and read the rival's own positioning, comparison pages and changelog. Never log in behind someone else's wall, scrape gated areas, or copy a rival's protected text, images or logos. Every claim about a rival needs a source and a date in its record, and unsourced claims are cut. Refresh cadence (a starting point, not a measured value): pricing and big feature changes every quarter, at once when a customer reports a change, and a full pass once a year.
7. **Add the search layer.** Target keywords per format are in the format file. Link related comparison pages to each other, link feature pages to the matching comparison, and make a hub page that lists everything. Answer questions like "what is the best alternative to X" as visible headings and short answers on the page. Do not add FAQ structured data for a rich result: Google's documentation says the FAQ rich result is no longer shown in search. Index pages and footer links: [site-structure.md](competitors/references/site-structure.md).
8. **Deliver.** Three outputs: the rival record file (YAML), the page content (URL, meta title and description, full copy by section, tables, calls to action), and a page set plan ordered by search volume.

## Checks before done

- Each rival claim has a public source and a check date, and nothing is copied from protected material.
- The rival's real strengths appear, and so do your own limits.
- No page is a bare checkmark grid; each dimension has explaining prose and a bottom line.
- The summary, who-it-suits and migration sections exist on every page.
- The plural format lists 4 to 7 genuine options.
- Pages read from the records, and the page set plan is ordered.
- English only, no em or en dashes.
