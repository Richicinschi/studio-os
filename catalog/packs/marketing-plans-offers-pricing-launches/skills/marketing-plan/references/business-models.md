# Business model types: what shifts inside the same 13 sections

The structure never changes; emphasis does. Pick the type closest to the main way the client earns, blend if hybrid, and state any deviation in the strategic frame.

## 1. B2B software
Subscription, often higher contract value, buyer differs from user.
- Acquisition: search and content (comparison pages, help content), professional-network presence, outbound email, events for large deals.
- Activation: signup, trial, first key action; or trial, demo, proof of concept when sales-led; guide users to value from empty screens.
- Retention: feature adoption, customer success for bigger accounts, lifecycle email on value moments.
- Referral: customer advocacy, integration co-marketing, review sites, champion-to-buyer growth.
- Revenue: expansion and net retention, seat and usage upgrades.
- Skills: cold-email, programmatic-seo, competitors, seo-audit, ai-seo, ads (professional network, search), emails, pricing.
- First budget: search and content, founder presence, nurture email, press via reporter queries.

## 2. Consumer subscription app
Low price per month, high volume, store listing as the front door.
- Acquisition: store optimisation first; paid social and store search ads once money exists; creators; press.
- Activation: onboarding is the key surface; time to value in minutes; paywall and trial design.
- Retention: lifecycle email and push (over-messaging causes churn), preference centre, win-back.
- Referral: built-in sharing (gift a month), two-sided rewards, creator ambassadors.
- Revenue: annual plan as default (lowers monthly revenue, raises LTV), tier clean-up, in-app upsells.
- Skills: onboarding, paywalls, emails, ads, ad-creative, referrals, pricing.
- First budget: store listing, onboarding rebuild, lifecycle email, founder social if they will be on camera.

## 3. Hardware plus software
Physical product as the wedge, software as recurring value, blended CAC.
- Acquisition: shop-page search, marketplace listing, press (physical goods photograph well), paid social with strong visuals.
- Activation: two moments to track, unboxing and software signup; the bridge between them; concierge setup for pricey units.
- Retention: separate post-purchase lifecycle, companion software stickiness, community.
- Referral: gifting, shareable design, reviews on shop and marketplace.
- Revenue: blended LTV (hardware margin plus subscription), bundles that include months of the software, annual default.
- Skills: seo-audit (shop pages), emails (both lifecycles), referrals with gifting, pricing (bundles), ads.
- First budget: shop-page work, post-purchase flow, bundle design, hardware-to-app activation check.

## 4. Marketplace
Two sides, network effects, liquidity first, take rate times volume.
- Acquisition: two funnels; supply by outbound and partnerships, demand by search, paid, content; city-by-city programmatic pages.
- Activation: supply's first listing and first reply; demand's first purchase or match.
- Retention: repeat transaction rate, share of listings active, buyer habit.
- Referral: same-side referrals work; cross-side weaker.
- Revenue: take-rate tuning, premium tier, lead fee versus transaction fee.
- Skills: programmatic-seo, cold-email, referrals, pricing.
- First budget: programmatic pages for one side, outbound to seed the scarce side, lifecycle for both.

## 5. Developer tool or open source
Skeptical technical buyer; docs are marketing.
- Acquisition: technical content, docs search, talks and community, repository presence, package discovery, technical forums.
- Activation: first build or integration; time to first working example; docs as onboarding.
- Retention: depth of integration, team adoption, project count.
- Referral: stars, forum recommendations, talks.
- Revenue: free to paid at usage limits, team and enterprise tiers, support tiers.
- Skills: programmatic-seo for docs, content-strategy, cold-email to engineering leads; little paid.
- First budget: docs and technical content, founder talks, repository polish, community posts.

## 6. Deep tech, science, clinical
Long cycles, proof burden, informed buyers, possible regulation.
- Acquisition: publications, conference talks, investor and adviser introductions, press on credibility hooks.
- Activation: pilots, high-touch setup, training sessions.
- Retention: heavy customer success, co-publication, practice community.
- Referral: academic or clinical references, panels, case studies with named institutions.
- Revenue: pilot to paid, institutional multi-year contracts, certification add-ons.
- Skills: product-marketing, sales-enablement, pricing, cold-email to named researchers, press and investor work; light classic marketing.
- First budget: academic outreach and talks, investor warm intros, key pilots, a case study or paper.

## 7. Commerce (one-off purchases)
Order value and repeat rate rule.
- Acquisition: paid social, shop search, marketplaces, creators.
- Activation: first purchase; cart recovery; checkout trust (reviews, returns, shipping).
- Retention: post-purchase flows, loyalty, email and SMS for reorders.
- Referral: gifting, refer-a-friend, reviews and customer content.
- Revenue: bundles and upsells for order value, reorder frequency, a subscription option.
- Skills: ads, ad-creative, emails, referrals, pricing.
- First budget: store optimisation, lifecycle email, creator seeding, small paid tests.

## Hybrids and misfits

Hybrids are normal: lead with the type matching the main revenue model and layer the other (hardware plus software with a clinical credibility layer: subscription patterns dominate, scientific-proof moves added). Media, social networks, event or property models fit nowhere: take the closest and state the deviation in section 2.
