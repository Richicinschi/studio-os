# Offer design

## When to use

The user sells something and the offer itself (not the page) may be the weak spot. Typical wordings: "build an offer", "my offer isn't converting", "should I add a guarantee", "how do I stack bonuses", "name this program", "put it on a payment plan", "turn my consulting into a product", "what do I do about a down-sell or an upsell offer".

This skill is built for services, agency work, courses, coaching, info products, sales-led B2B contracts and paid-traffic sales pages. Better copy on a weak offer pays off slowly, while a stronger offer with ordinary copy tends to move at once, so many "we need better copy" requests are really offer requests.

Hand over to a neighbour when it fits better:
- Self-serve software with tiers, or a marketplace: pricing first (tiers, freemium, value metric, packaging). Use this skill only as a supplement.
- The price level itself: pricing.
- The page that presents the offer: copywriting. The conversion path around it: cro.
- The in-app upgrade prompt: paywalls.
- The moment of release: launch. The warm-up emails: emails.
- The deck or one-pager for a sales call: sales-enablement.
- Why buyers are swayed or put off: marketing-psychology.

## How it works

Voice: opinionated, but honest. Build the offer well, not loudly.

1. **Load context.** Before asking anything, check for the product marketing context file at `.agents/product-marketing.md`, then `.claude/product-marketing.md`, then the older name `product-marketing-context.md` in either folder. If found, read it and ask only for what it lacks or what is specific to this task.
2. **Place the business and write the current offer down.** Which type is it: service, course, coaching, info product, software, agency, B2B? Write the present offer in plain words: name, price, what is included, guarantee, deadline. Scattered notes count; gather them. Type-specific shapes are in [formats.md](offers/references/formats.md).
3. **Score the value equation.** Rate four levers from 1 to 10: the outcome the buyer wants, their belief it will happen to them, the wait for results, and the work and risk they carry. The lowest score is the constraint. A "lower the price" request usually means one of these is weak. Details and a worked case: [value-equation.md](offers/references/value-equation.md).
4. **Audit the six parts.** Core deliverable, bonuses, guarantee, scarcity or urgency, name, price with payment structure. Mark which are missing or weak. Weak offers usually lack bonuses, carry the wrong guarantee, or have fake or no scarcity; embarrassing ones over-promise or fake a timer. Part-by-part guidance: [anatomy.md](offers/references/anatomy.md).
5. **Fix one thing per round.** Pick the lever or part with the biggest gap and change only that. Do not rebuild everything in one round.
6. **Draft the change.** Use the matching file: [bonuses.md](offers/references/bonuses.md) for bonus stacks, [guarantees.md](offers/references/guarantees.md) for guarantee types and wording, [scarcity.md](offers/references/scarcity.md) for honest limits. Naming, payment plans, down-sells and upsells are covered in anatomy.md.
7. **Be honest about the lift.** One change usually helps modestly and nobody can say the number in advance. Anyone promising a fivefold jump is selling something. Two rounds on different levers can add up; measure each. Do not invent expected results.
8. **Plan the next round.** Name the next lowest lever, so the work continues in order. Worked sketches: [examples.md](offers/references/examples.md) (illustrations, not results).

## Banned wording

When drafting offer language, avoid:
- Hype words such as game-changing, revolutionary, disruptive, next-level, 10x.
- "Secret", "hidden", or "what they do not want you to know".
- "Limited time" with no real end date.
- "Worth $X" with no comparable price behind it.
- "100% guaranteed" with no stated conditions.

Use specific numbers, named customers (with permission), concrete outcomes and real timelines. Specifics beat superlatives.

## What not to do

- Do not use fake scarcity: no resetting countdown, no invented "few left". It lifts one sale and costs trust afterwards.
- Do not promise what the guarantee cannot pay for; a refund-plus-extra on a result you cannot control eats the margin, and one public failure hurts more than the promise gained.
- Do not inflate bonuses: a huge stated value beside a small price is read as a trick by careful buyers. Treat bonuses as an addition, not an exaggeration.
- Do not dress a serious product in get-rich styling (gold logos, "secret method"). It reads as a scam to the wrong room.

## Checks before done

- Every limit shown (seats, dates, price rises) is real and will be enforced; no resetting timers, no fixed "few left".
- No proof is invented: testimonials, numbers, case studies and lift figures all trace to something that exists. Where there is none, the draft says so and uses a pilot or narrow guarantee.
- The guarantee survives a stress test: what if one buyer in ten claims it?
- Bonus values are defensible and total under about twice the core price (a starting point, not a measured value).
- The tone fits the audience; no get-rich styling on a serious product.
- No banned wording remains.
- The user knows what was changed, which lever it targets, and what to measure next.
