# Team model

Use this at step 7 (drafting section 11 and the sections around it) and step 8. ops-stack.md covers what gets done; this file covers who does it.

## What the plan must decide

1. What stays in the company and what is contracted.
2. Who owns each of three marketing functions: demand and funnel; message, launches and pricing communication; trust-building content. One person may hold several.
3. Whether anyone owns strategy.
4. The shape, title and level of the first hire.
5. Which kind of outside help fits.
6. The inside and outside split at the company's stage.
7. When the second hire comes and when agencies deepen.

## The core rule

Keep strategy inside: customer understanding, positioning, the core product and the memory of what was tried. Outsource specialist craft, burst capacity, well-scoped programs and a fresh view from outside. Two mistakes recur. One is hiring a junior doer first, which gives scattered tactics. The other is paying an agency to set strategy, which gives advice nobody inside can act on. A permanent hire takes months to reach full speed; an experienced contractor can deliver within weeks.

## If strategy has no owner

- Nobody owns strategy: the first move is to hire one.
- Strategy exists but hands are missing: the first move is a contractor or a small niche agency.

Record this in section 9 as the opening move.

## The first hire

Prefer two deep skills plus broad competence over one deep skill. Useful pairings: message plus growth; message plus content; growth plus content. A lone specialist in paid media, search or design is the wrong first hire, because that person needs a strategic owner above them.

Seniority ladder: individual contributor, lead, director or head, vice president, chief. Start low so there is room to grow. A mid or senior contributor, or a lead, is right. Do not give the first hire an executive title.

Expected duties: define positioning, choose channels from data, write the messaging framework, build systems that last, and manage outside resources.

## Outside help, best fit first for an early company

1. Individual contractors.
2. Small niche agencies.
3. Small generalist agencies.
4. Large agencies, only when spend is big enough to get senior attention.

Test a brief by its specifics. Weak: "make us some ads." Better: "12 ad variants across 3 audience groups by the 15th, judged against stated click targets." Every brief needs a concrete scope, deliverables, deadlines and targets. These figures are an illustration.

## Working with vendors

- Before the start: agree objectives, timeline, channels and metrics, and write down how you will work together.
- During: weekly tactical check-ins, monthly strategy check-ins, shared data, review against the metrics.
- Warning signs: scope drifting beyond their skill, staff turnover, missed deadlines, silence, reports that show no value.

## Split by company stage

- Before product-market fit: the work is discovery. One or two strategic people, short contractor commitments, agency work by project.
- After fit: a small strategic core, a mix of contractors and agencies, longer relationships.
- At scale: a larger strategic team that coordinates specialist agencies and a trusted pool of contractors.

Tie the second hire and the depth of agency work to the funding tier (funding-tiers.md). If a function is unsettled, whether to hire or buy it is a top-three open decision.

## Rules to apply

- Do not hand positioning to a vendor.
- Do not use an agency to dodge a strategy conversation.
- Do not open a permanent role for work that ends in about six months; use a contractor.
- Judge a team by what it produces, not by how many people it has.
