# Scoring rubrics

Score each dimension from 0 to 10. A 0 means "could not assess", not "bad".

Every threshold in this file (rating counts, languages, update age, density, screenshot counts, weights) is this skill's own scoring guideline, a starting point, not a measured value or a store rule.

## Brand tiers

| Tier | Signals |
|---|---|
| Dominant | Everyday household name, over a million ratings, top 10 in its category. People search the brand, not generic terms. |
| Established | Well known in its field, over 100K ratings, solid organic installs. |
| Challenger | Still building awareness, under 100K ratings. Most apps. |

How the tier shifts scoring:
- Dominant: a brand-only title can score 8 or more (the brand is the keyword); description is judged on conversion quality alone; lifestyle imagery can equal captioned UI shots; no video is fine when awareness is near total or the product is hard to show in 30 seconds; generic release notes at weekly or faster cadence can score 8 or more; missing events for a utility app is no penalty; localization is judged against the real market footprint.
- Established: brand-first titles are fine if they still hold 1 or 2 keywords; give strategic choices the benefit of the doubt; everything else is scored as normal.
- Challenger: strict. Every character, screenshot and keyword counts.

## 1. Title and subtitle (20%)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Brand plus a strong keyword in the title, complementary terms in the subtitle, no repeated words, near-full use of space, purpose obvious at a glance |
| 7-8 | Good keywords, 5 or more unused characters, clear purpose |
| 5-6 | Keywords present but badly placed, some repetition, purpose fairly clear |
| 3-4 | Brand-only or generic title, weak or missing subtitle |
| 1-2 | No keyword plan, purpose unclear, much wasted space |

"Near full" means within 3 characters of the limit. Also check characters used against the limit (title 30; subtitle 30 on Apple, short description 80 on Google; near full is 27 or more of 30, 77 or more of 80); main keyword in title; keywords duplicated between title and subtitle; filler words eating space; claims that risk rejection on Apple.

## 2. Description (15%)

Apple (not indexed, so judge persuasion):
- 9-10: first 3 lines state the value, structure covers features, benefits, proof and a call to action, promotional text is used, easy to scan.
- 7-8: good opening and structure, scanning or call to action could improve.
- 5-6: generic opener, some structure, no proof or call to action.
- 3-4: wall of text, no value above the fold, no promotional text.
- 1-2: boilerplate.

Google Play (indexed, so judge both):
- 9-10: keywords in the first 3 sentences, natural 2 to 3% density, formatting used, clear sections, strong call to action.
- 7-8: decent keywords and structure, density at 1 to 2% or 3 to 4%.
- 5-6: sparse (under 1%) or stuffed (over 5%), weak structure.
- 3-4: no visible keyword plan, poor formatting.
- 1-2: minimal text.

Check the text visible before "read more", benefit framing over feature lists, proof (downloads, awards, press), call to action, density (count target terms divided by total words), formatting (Google) and promotional text (Apple).

## 3. Visual assets (25%)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | 8 to 10 screenshots with clear one-benefit captions that read as a story, a preview video, a distinctive icon |
| 7-8 | 6 or 7 captioned screenshots and a good icon, but no video or some unclear captions |
| 5-6 | 5 or more screenshots with weak or no captions, plain icon, no video |
| 3-4 | 3 or 4 screenshots, no captions, generic icon |
| 1-2 | Under 3 screenshots or raw unedited UI, poor icon |

Check: count (5 minimum, 8 to 10 ideal); one message per caption, 5 to 7 words; the first 3 screenshots; video quality; icon without text and with bold shapes; Google feature graphic; story flow; localized visuals in non-English markets; keywords in Apple captions.

## 4. Ratings and reviews (20%)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | 4.5 or higher, 10K+ ratings, positive recent reviews, replies to criticism, steady flow |
| 7-8 | 4.0 to 4.4, 1K+ ratings, mostly positive, some replies |
| 5-6 | 3.5 to 3.9, 500+ ratings, mixed, no replies |
| 3-4 | 3.0 to 3.4, under 500 ratings, recurring complaints |
| 1-2 | Under 3.0, few ratings, no engagement |

Check average, count, sentiment of the last 5 to 10 visible reviews, complaint themes (crashes, price, usability), reply quality, trend and recency.

## 5. Metadata and freshness (10%)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Updated within a month, 10 or more languages, best-fit category, events or promotional content live, data safety complete |
| 7-8 | Updated within 2 months, 5 or more languages, a fitting category, and a data safety section |
| 5-6 | Updated within 3 months, 2 to 4 languages |
| 3-4 | Updated 3 to 6 months ago, 1 or 2 languages, doubtful category |
| 1-2 | Over 6 months stale, single language, poor category |

Also check age rating, release notes quality and the privacy label.

## 6. Conversion signals (10%)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Value clear before install, transparent pricing, visible proof, strong download range, credible developer |
| 7-8 | Clear value and pricing, some proof |
| 5-6 | Weak value statement, unclear pricing or heavy in-app purchases, little proof |
| 3-4 | Unclear what the user gets, confusing pricing, low traction |
| 1-2 | No value shown, suspicious pricing, looks abandoned |

Check price clarity, purchase names, Google download range, developer reputation, featured or editor badges, press and awards, the developer's other apps and privacy transparency.

## Final score

Total = title x 0.20 + description x 0.15 + visuals x 0.25 + ratings x 0.20 + metadata x 0.10 + conversion x 0.10, then multiply by 10.

Worked example: title 5, description 8, visuals 6, ratings 7, metadata 9, conversion 4 gives 1.0 + 1.2 + 1.5 + 1.4 + 0.9 + 0.4 = 6.4, so 64 out of 100, grade C.
