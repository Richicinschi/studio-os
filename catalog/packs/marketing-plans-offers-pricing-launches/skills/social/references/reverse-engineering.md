# Learning from what already works

Guessing is slow. Study strong accounts in your niche, pull out the patterns, then say them in your own voice. This is pattern learning, never copying text.

## Method in six moves

The counts below (accounts, posts, top share) are the skill's own starting points, not measured values.

### 1. Pick the accounts
Choose 10 to 20 creators who post at least 3 times a week, get high engagement for their size, reach the same buyers as you, and include both big names and rising ones. Ways to find them: keyword searches on the platform, the accounts your buyers follow and reply to, audience-research tools, and names that newsletters in your field feature.

### 2. Collect posts
Aim for 500 or more posts. Capture: text, engagement counts (likes, comments, shares, saves), format (text, carousel, video, image), day and time, the opening line, the call to action, the topic. Small sets can be copied into a spreadsheet by hand. Scraping services and export features exist, but many sites forbid automated collection: read the terms first, never use someone's login to do it, and ask the owner before paying for a service.

### 3. Analyse
Numbers: rank by engagement rate, isolate the top 10 percent, compare formats, days and topics. Reading: which openers recur, how long the best posts are, which emotions show up, which formats repeat, which topics get saved or shared. Questions to answer: average length of top posts, most common hook type among winners, which call to action draws comments, which topics get saved.

### 4. Write the playbook
Hook patterns, each with a pattern, a sample and the reason it works:
- "I [unusual move] and [unexpected effect]". Sample: "I stopped answering email before noon and closed more deals." Why: a curiosity gap plus a contrary move.
- "[Exact number] [things] that [result]:". Sample: "9 onboarding mistakes that cost me 140 users:" Why: precision and fear of loss.
- A flat, bold claim. Sample: "Dashboards are where good ideas go to die." Why: breaks the pattern and invites argument.

Format patterns: carousel (hook slide, problem, solution steps, call to action); thread (hook, promise, delivery, recap, call to action); story post (hook, setup, conflict, resolution, lesson).

Call-to-action patterns: ask a question ("What would you add?"); ask for a side ("Agree?"); ask for a share; ask for a save.

### 5. Add your voice
- Sound like a smart friend passing on a discovery: "I found that..." beats "You should...".
- Specific beats vague: "$3,412" not "good revenue"; "31 days" not "a while"; "1,906 people" not "many".
- Short, then breathe, then land: one idea per line, white space, rhythm of short, short, longer. A long run-on sentence about slowly learning to focus becomes: "I built it wrong for two years. Then it clicked. Do fewer things. Do them well."
- Be honest about the feeling when it is real, vulnerability included, and tie it to the lesson. Open from the feeling, then the lesson: "My stomach dropped when I hit send on the new price list." beats "Here is what I learned about pricing."

### 6. Turn attention into action
Soft: newsletter link in the profile or a comment, a free resource offered in a follow-up comment, a "comment a word and I will send it" prompt (only if you really send it, and only to people who asked), a profile with a clear next step. Direct: link in a comment rather than the body on LinkedIn, product mentions inside useful content, case-study posts, and a rare "message me if you want help".

## The formula
1. Find what works, do not guess.
2. Extract hooks, formats and calls to action.
3. Put your own voice on top.
4. Test, then adjust from your own numbers.

## Checklist
- [ ] 10 to 20 accounts chosen
- [ ] 500 or more posts collected within each site's terms
- [ ] Ranked by engagement rate
- [ ] 10 hook patterns, 5 format patterns, 5 call-to-action patterns written
- [ ] Voice notes (specific, short, emotional)
- [ ] Template library built
- [ ] Tracking of your own posts in place
