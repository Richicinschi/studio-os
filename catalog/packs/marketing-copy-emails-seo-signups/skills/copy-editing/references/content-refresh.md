# Refreshing published content

Live pages age: figures go stale, examples date, the voice drifts away from today's brand, and search needs change. A refresh applies editing discipline to something already published.

## Signs a page needs one

- Traffic is falling on a page that used to do well.
- Figures or data look old (a starting point is about a year, not a measured value).
- The product changed, so features, prices or positioning on the page are wrong.
- Competitors have updated their own versions of the same page.
- You want to be cited by AI search tools, which tend to prefer current pages (the ai-seo skill covers this).

## Six passes

1. **Freshness.** Update dates, numbers and examples. Replace year-specific wording with current data. Remove mentions of retired features or tools.
2. **Accuracy.** Recheck every claim. Confirm each link still resolves. Make sure prices and feature descriptions match the product today.
3. **Voice.** Compare the tone with the brand as it sounds now; older pages often speak for an earlier version of the company.
4. **Search.** Has the intent behind the query shifted? Are there new questions or terms to answer? Show a clear "last updated" date.
5. **Proof.** Add newer quotes, case studies or data that did not exist at first publication.
6. **Structure.** Add things that help scanning: comparison tables, an FAQ block, short lists.

## Refresh or rewrite?

- Core message holds, details are stale: refresh (facts, figures, examples).
- Only stats and links are out of date: light refresh.
- Brand voice has moved a lot: refresh plus a voice rewrite.
- The angle or the audience has changed: full rewrite.
- The page structure no longer fits what searchers want: full rewrite.

## How often

These intervals are the skill's own starting points, not measured values. Adjust them using each page's own traffic and change history.

- Pricing and product pages: each quarter, or whenever prices or features change.
- Comparison and alternatives pages: every 3 to 6 months, since competitors move fast.
- Popular blog posts: every 6 months.
- Evergreen guides: yearly, sooner if traffic drops.
- Low-traffic pages: only when the data shows a chance worth taking.

Show the user the diff of what you changed and why; do not publish the page yourself.
