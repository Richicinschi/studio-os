# Common copy problems and their fixes

Format: what you see, then what to do.

- **Feature wall.** The text lists what the product does and never why that matters. After each feature add "which means..." and finish with the gain for the reader.
- **Corporate speak.** Phrases like "align stakeholders to unlock value across verticals". Ask how a person would say it to a friend, then write that.
- **Weak opening.** Starts with company history or a vague claim. Open with the reader's problem or the result they want.
- **Buried call to action.** The ask arrives after a long build-up, or is hard to find. Make it obvious, put it early, and repeat it.
- **No proof.** "Our clients are delighted" with nothing behind it. Add a named quote, a number with its source, or a case reference. If there is none, soften the claim.
- **Generic claim.** "We empower companies to reach their potential." Say who, how, and by how much.
- **Mixed audiences.** Tries to address everyone and reaches no one. Choose one reader and write to them directly.
- **Feature overload.** Every capability is listed and the reader drowns. Keep the few benefits (a starting point is 3 to 5, not a measured value) that matter most to this audience and move the rest elsewhere.
