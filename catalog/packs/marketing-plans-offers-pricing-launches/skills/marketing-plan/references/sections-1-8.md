# Sections 1 to 8: what to draft

Title block for the compiled plan: `# <Client> marketing plan v1`, then prepared by, for whom, date, status ("draft v1, for team review"). Close with one italic line naming client, version, author, date.

Section 1 is written after all others, but placed first.

## 1. Executive summary (400 to 700 words)

Must be pasteable into a board or investor note without edits. Four parts:
1. One sentence on what the plan optimises for, specific to this client and stage (never "more revenue").
2. Three bets ranked by leverage, one paragraph each. A bet is a firm thesis about where money and attention go.
3. What twelve months plausibly looks like, as bullets an investor can read.
4. About six first-quarter moves, numbered.
Use names and numbers, match the client's voice, name tradeoffs.

## 2. Strategic frame (800 to 1,500 words)

Orient a new hire on one page. Subsections: the company in one sentence; the category it claims (new, redefined or contested) in two or three sentences; who it is for, in four to six bullets (fit, stated versus real problem, what they actually buy); business-model logic (how money is made, acquisition economics theory, any compounding channel); brand voice that cannot be broken (word lists, CTA rules, tone, method). Surface what already exists; do not invent positioning. Contradictions between materials go to the open decisions.

## 3. Current state (1,000 to 2,000 words)

- Team table: person, role, marketing surface. Name the gaps and when a hire becomes necessary.
- Budget: paid, tools with cost, retainers, headcount, blended CAC, spend as a share of ARR (set against the person's own ceiling, using the method in budget-math.md), the funding tier, and what the first 90 days must achieve without future money.
- Growth phase: name it and its binding constraint.
- Done (asset, status, leverage), in flight (item, status, blocker), stuck (issue, cost of waiting, action). Stuck items are the best first-weeks targets.
- Rubric snapshot: 17 rows, total out of 85, plus the shape. Mark "scored from materials" when not from a prior audit.
Honesty beats polish; founders see through softened numbers.

## Sections 4 to 8: one shared shape

Each has: current state (short), the plan as numbered moves of three to six sentences, 90-day moves, 12-month outlook by quarter, skills and tools (never skipped). Tag each move with a stage; if it spans two, see funnel-stages.md.

**4. Acquisition (1,000 to 1,800 words).** Channels live, planned, skipped with reasons. Typical moves: SEO and content (reference an SEO plan if one exists), store listing optimisation for consumer apps, founder-led social, press amplification of the credibility anchor, events, commerce surface (shop, marketplace, retail), B2B sales support (case studies, partner pages, vertical content), a paid layer held until the tier allows it.

**5. Activation (800 to 1,500).** Typical moves: fix broken gates first, rebuild or test onboarding, rewrite the store listing (cross-link to 4), decide when onboarding emails ship versus waiting for a stable product, review paywall and pricing (shared with 8).

**6. Retention (800 to 1,500).** Lifecycle flows (post-purchase, lapsed, win-back), preference centres, reconcile churn definitions that differ across tools, hardware-to-software paths, annual-plan default tests (shared with 8).

**7. Referral (500 to 1,200).** Launch the ambassador or affiliate program with whoever is already volunteering, share-after-value moments in the product, founder as first referrer, expert or certified-partner networks as a long game, gifting flows.

**8. Revenue (500 to 1,200).** Pricing audit (charged versus listed), annual default test, hardware-plus-software bundles, shop page work, B2B case studies and sales material, long-term value pools flagged but not executed this year. Include the unit economics table:

| Metric | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Average monthly revenue per customer | | from billing |
| Blended CAC | | all marketing cost, not paid only |
| Annual retention | | 1 minus annual churn |
| Rough LTV | | monthly revenue x 12 / annual churn |
| LTV / CAC | | set your own target; 3 is a common starting point, not a measured value |

Unknowns here become the top open decision.

Skills lists per stage are in ops-stack.md.
