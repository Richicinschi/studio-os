# Personalization, segmentation and testing

## Personalization

- Merge fields: first name with a safe fallback such as "friend", company name for B2B, and facts from real usage such as plan or activity.
- Dynamic blocks: swap content by segment, by behavior or by lifecycle stage.
- Triggered sends: emails fired by an action (a feature used, a milestone reached, a stretch of silence) are usually more relevant than ones fired by the calendar. Prefer them where the data exists.
- Never personalize with data you are unsure of. A wrong detail costs more trust than a generic line.

## Segmentation

- By behavior: opened or not, clicked or not, active or dormant.
- By stage: trial or paid, new or long-standing, engaged or at risk.
- By profile: industry or role (B2B), use case or goal, company size.

Segment when the message would genuinely differ. A split that leads to the same email is wasted effort.

## Testing

What to test, in rough order of impact:
1. Subject lines.
2. Send time.
3. Email length.
4. Call-to-action position and wording.
5. Depth of personalization.
6. Gaps between emails in the sequence.

How to test:
- Change one variable per test.
- Wait for a sample large enough to mean something and for statistical significance before declaring a winner.
- Write down what you learned so it is not re-tested later.

## Metrics

- Open rate: opens divided by delivered, per send. Count it over the last several sends of the same flow.
- Click rate: unique clickers divided by delivered, over the same period.
- Unsubscribe rate: unsubscribes divided by delivered, over the same period.
- Conversion rate: defined by the sequence goal.
- Revenue per email: where the flow sells something.

There is no trustworthy outside benchmark here: lists, products and mail clients differ too much. Measure your own baseline per flow (what to count, over what period, compared with what), then judge a change against that baseline. Mail privacy features inflate open counts, so lean on clicks and conversions.
