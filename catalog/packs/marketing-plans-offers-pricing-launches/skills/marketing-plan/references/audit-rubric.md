# Current-state audit: 17 rows, 0 to 5 each

This rubric is the scoring lens for section 3. Score from the materials, write a one-line reason per row, total out of 85, then 2 to 4 sentences on the shape.

Two ways in:
- **From materials:** decks, content audits, a voice document, positioning work, a kickoff transcript, store and analytics snapshots. Label the section "scored from materials" so the team can correct you with better data.
- **From an existing scored audit in any format:** copy those scores; do not redo them. Note the date it was scored and flag rows where things have changed since (new flows live, new content, repositioning), updating only where you hold current evidence.

Anchors: 0 means absent; 2 means present but weak or inconsistent; 4 means good with small gaps; 5 means distinctive and operating at top level. Rows where the anchor differs are described.

## Reading the shape

After the table write: "High in X and Y, low in Z. That shape is the gap the plan closes; section N is longest because that is where the gap is widest." Common shapes:
- Strong voice and messaging, weak distribution (high 1, 2, 11; low 14, 15, 16): emphasise acquisition and paid-readiness.
- Strong acquisition, weak conversion (high 7, 15, 16; low 3, 5, 8, 12): emphasise activation and revenue.
- Strong conversion, weak retention (high 3, 8, 12; low 9, 13): emphasise retention.
- Strong product, little else (high only 1, 2): pre-marketing; first quarter is foundation fixes.
- Strong recent revenue, weak compounding (high 10, 12, 15; low 7, 16, referral mechanics): build compounding channels before scaling paid.

## Subjectivity

Data-backed: SEO, ads, email lifecycle, onboarding. Semi-objective (artifacts you can inspect): pricing, CRO, conversion pages, sales material. Judgment calls: positioning, messaging, customer research, content. For judgment rows put the reasoning in the note so the team can push back.
