## When to use

Typical wordings: "how do I get press", "pitch a journalist", "build a media list", "write a press release", "newsjack this story", "answer this HARO query", "get featured in tech press", "I want an op-ed or guest article placed", "set up a press page or press kit".

This skill covers earned media only: finding journalists, pitching stories, reacting to the news, answering press requests, and making the company easy to find for reporters. It is not about pull requests.

Neighbours, and where each takes over:
- Startup, SaaS and AI directory listings: `directory-submissions`.
- A product launch plan as a whole: `launch`.
- Day-to-day social engagement: `social`.
- Cold outreach to prospects or buyers rather than reporters: `cold-email`.

The five reference files hold the detail. Open only the one that matches the request.

## How it works

You act as a specialist in earned media for software products. The aim is coverage by journalists, podcasts and newsletters, won efficiently and with respect for the people being pitched.

1. **Read the context first.** If a product marketing context file exists (`.agents/product-marketing.md`, or the older `product-marketing-context.md`), read it before asking anything. Ask only for what it does not cover.
2. **Hold the right frame.** PR multiplies distribution; it does not replace it. A big placement rarely brings paying users directly. It brings backlinks, credibility, a chance of being cited by AI assistants, and something to show in sales talks. Treat a reporter like a customer: be specific, useful and quick, and never talk about yourself. The story is the trend, the numbers, the conflict or the person; the product is only the evidence. On reactive work a decent pitch sent early beats a perfect one sent late.
3. **Decide whether PR is worth doing now.** Go ahead when there is a real story (own data, a firm opinion, a milestone, a customer with a sharp before and after, a new angle on a live topic), when a founder can give interview time, and when there is a page that turns attention into something (a press page, post or release). Hold off when the only news is that the product exists, when nobody can keep pitching for several weeks in a row (plan the weeks you can really give), or when you cannot say who the audience is. A reporter will ask who reads the piece because of this; if you cannot answer, they cannot either.
4. **Pick the mix.** There are four modes and most teams lean on one. Run at least three.

   | Mode | What it is | Effort | Time to coverage |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Reactive | Add your view to a story that is already moving | Low to medium | Fast, while the story is live |
   | Proactive | Build a list and pitch original stories | High | Slow; log your own pitch-to-coverage time |
   | Inbound | Answer reporters' public calls for sources | Low | Set by each request's deadline |
   | Owned | Press page and kit so reporters can find you | One-off set-up | Not applicable |

   Detail files:
   - Reactive: [newsjack-loop.md](public-relations/newsjack-loop.md)
   - Choosing and scoring reporters: [building-a-media-list.md](public-relations/building-a-media-list.md)
   - Writing pitches, subject lines, follow-ups: [pitch-writing.md](public-relations/pitch-writing.md)
   - Press-request platforms: [inbound-requests.md](public-relations/inbound-requests.md)
   - Which outlets, newsletters and podcasts: [where-to-pitch.md](public-relations/where-to-pitch.md)
5. **Set up the owned base once.** A press page (`/press` or `/newsroom`) should hold: a short company description ready to paste; founder bios with downloadable high-resolution photos; a logo pack (SVG and PNG, light and dark, with usage rules); high-resolution product screenshots; a list of past coverage; basic facts such as founding date, team size and funding if public; a plain email address for press, not a form; and earlier announcements. Put one line at the top promising a reply within 24 hours, then really reply within 24 hours.
6. **Run the quality bar before any pitch is shown as ready.** The limits below are the skill's own starting points, not measured values. Every answer must be yes:
   - The reporter covers this beat (check their last 5 pieces).
   - There is a news hook: something just happened or is about to.
   - They could write the whole story from the email alone (numbers, quotes, a named customer, a contact).
   - The subject line could predict the headline.
   - The pitch is under 150 words.
   - No hype words (revolutionary, game-changing, disruptive, synergy).
   - The ask is clear: interview, embargo, exclusive or quote.

   One no means the pitch is not shown as ready and is not sent. Never invent a quote, a statistic, a customer or a credential; use only what the user supplied or what can be sourced.
7. **Hand over drafts, do not send them.** Produce drafts one at a time for review. Nothing goes out, is posted, or is submitted to a platform without the owner's word, and there is no mass pitching: never the same angle to many reporters, never a bulk send.
8. **Measure.** Track coverage count per month, the strength of the sites that linked, referral visits from each piece, rise in searches for the brand, whether AI assistants now name the brand, and sales conversations that mention an article (the one that matters for revenue). Ignore advertising value equivalency; it is a vanity figure.

### Common requests

- "Newsjack this story": score it, write 2 or 3 angles, choose the best, draft the pitch (newsjack-loop).
- "Find reporters on this beat": run the discovery checklist, research recent articles with a browser tool if one is available, build a scored list (building-a-media-list).
- "What is worth pitching this week": combine recent milestones, news cycles that are live and any data collected; score each candidate on the quality bar.
- "Answer this reporter request": use the response template and keep it under 200 words (inbound-requests).
- "Build my press page": follow step 5; it takes an afternoon and can sit for a year.

## Checks before done

- Every pitch passed the quality bar in step 6, and every claim in it can be traced to the user's material.
- No invented quote, figure, name or credential anywhere.
- Nothing was sent, posted or submitted; drafts are waiting for the owner's word.
- Text is English only with no em or en dashes, and none of the banned hype words.
- The right neighbour skill was named where the request left PR.
