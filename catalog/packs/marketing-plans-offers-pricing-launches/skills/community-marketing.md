## When to use

Typical wordings: "build a community", "community strategy", "grow our community", "Discord community", "Slack community", "forum strategy", "community engagement", "user community", "ambassador program", "community flywheel", "turn customers into evangelists", "get more word-of-mouth".

Neighbours, and where each takes over:
- Referral rewards and incentive mechanics: the referral skill in this pack.
- Keeping customers from leaving (cancel flows, win-back): the churn skill in this pack.
- Posts and content for social channels: the social skill in this pack.
- Interviews, surveys and member language research: the customer research skill in this pack.

Studio rules for everything here: English only, no em or en dashes. Never suggest fake accounts, sock puppets or staged "organic" praise. Follow each platform's own rules on promotion and self-posting. Nothing is posted, DMed or announced publicly until the owner has said so; draft it and stop.

## How it works

1. Check for a saved product marketing context file in the project (`.agents/product-marketing.md`, or the older `product-marketing-context.md`). Read it first and ask only for what it does not cover.
2. Get the situation, one short round of questions at most: what the product is and who uses it; the platform in play or under consideration; the stage (pre-launch, under 100 members, 100 to 1,000, scaling, established); the main goal (retention, activation, word-of-mouth, support deflection, product feedback, revenue); who the ideal member is and what they want; whether existing users can seed it; how many hours a week someone can give. If answers are missing, assume something sensible and label it as an assumption.
3. Settle three foundations before any tactics.
   - Identity: members stay for who they are among peers, not for the product. A cycling-repair forum is about people who fix their own bikes. State in one sentence what identity this community reinforces.
   - Member value first: every touchpoint must answer what the member gets. Candidates are knowledge, peers they cannot meet elsewhere, status, a say in the roadmap, career visibility.
   - The flywheel: members join, get value, take part, produce help or content, and that brings in new members. Test each decision against whether it speeds this loop up or slows it down.
4. Pick the playbook that matches the goal and stage, and follow it from [playbooks.md](community-marketing/playbooks.md): launching from zero, growing an existing community, an ambassador program, or community-led support. If the platform is undecided, use the comparison in [platforms-and-health.md](community-marketing/platforms-and-health.md).
5. Deliver one concrete artefact, chosen by the request:
   - strategy doc: platform, identity, core loop, 90-day plan
   - channel architecture: each channel with its purpose and posting guidance
   - new member journey: pinned welcome, DM template, first-week prompts
   - ritual calendar: weekly and monthly recurring threads and events
   - ambassador brief: criteria, benefits, outreach template, tracking plan
   - health audit: current numbers, diagnosis, the top 3 fixes
6. For an existing community, measure weekly with the signals and warning signs in [platforms-and-health.md](community-marketing/platforms-and-health.md) and base the audit on them.

## Checks before done

- The output is specific enough to act on today; no "be consistent" or "add value" without a named action, owner and date.
- Every assumption from step 2 is listed.
- A single weekly core-loop action is named and has a public reward.
- No tactic relies on fake accounts, bought engagement, or unsolicited promotion that the platform forbids.
- Anything meant to go out publicly (welcome post, outreach DM, announcement) is marked as a draft awaiting the owner's word.
- Health signals are compared with the community's own earlier weeks, and any warning-sign threshold is labelled as a starting point.
