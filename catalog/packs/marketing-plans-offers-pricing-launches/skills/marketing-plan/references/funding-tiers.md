# Funding tiers

Use this at step 6 (pick the lens), step 8 (next funding milestone and what it unlocks) and step 9 (say what changes when the next round closes; never plan paid money before it exists). The five stages below are described by what they allow. Dollar amounts are not given: derive them from the client's own numbers using the method here, and replace any figure from another file that conflicts.

## What the plan must decide

1. The client's tier today, and so what the plan may assume.
2. What the next round unlocks: paid test budget, tooling, hires, capabilities.
3. Category adjustments.
4. The tooling lines for the lowest tier.
5. How tier-dependent moves are labelled.
6. How to handle a mismatch between what the founder wants and the tier.
7. When to re-plan.

## The tiers

1. Pre-seed or bootstrapped. Organic only, no paid, no hires; fractional help at most. Spend is tooling. Write the budget statement in your own words, such as "current monthly marketing budget: [amount], tools only, no paid".
2. Seed closed. A first paid pilot, a first dedicated hire (lifecycle or content; growth if the plan leans on paid), a first public launch, press tied to the funding news.
3. Seed being deployed. Paid across two or three channels, a designer (possibly fractional), a second manager, full lifecycle flows, a structured launch routine.
4. Series A. Paid at greater intensity on channels already proven, brand campaigns, early international and vertical expansion, attribution work. Hires: a performance lead, a content lead, a permanent designer; agencies are possible.
5. Series B and later. Brand work at category scale, PR firms, sponsorships, bought media. A marketing organisation of ten or more, led by an executive, with brand, growth, content, design, PR and regional leads. An executive title is fine here because it comes long after the first hire (team-model.md).

At each tier the planner's role shifts: hands-on first, then strategy and oversight, then adviser or absent.

## Method for the money at each tier

Tooling: list the tools the plan needs, read each price from the vendor's pricing page on the day you write the plan, and add them up.

Paid test, one rule for every tier: ask the person for their monthly ceiling, or accept a range they choose. Then check it against a floor you compute. The floor is the spend that produces enough conversions to read a result in one measurement period.

Example (invented numbers): you want about 30 conversions to judge a channel; the expected cost per conversion is 60, taken from measured data; uncertainty buffer 50 percent. Floor = 30 x 60 x 1.5 = 2,700 a month. If the person's ceiling is 2,000, the test cannot be read in a month, so extend the period or narrow the test. If the ceiling is 8,000, choose a figure in 2,700 to 8,000 and say why.

The same method gives the second-tier test budget wherever it is quoted. Do not restate a different floor elsewhere in the plan; point to the tier by name.

## Category adjustments

Consumer app, business software, hardware plus software, deep technology (scientific or clinical), marketplace and developer tools each move the paid floor and the channel weighting. For a consumer app, set the floor from the client's measured cost per install and trial-to-paid rate over at least one full trial period. For business software, use measured cost per qualified lead.

## Labelling and recalibrating

State the tier in section 3. In sections 4 to 8, a lowest-tier plan contains only moves doable now, or moves explicitly labelled future with the tier and what holds them, such as "held until the round closes". Each quarter in the 12-month section names its tier, and a tier change triggers a re-plan. Section 11 holds a capability table: today against after the next round.

## When wants and tier disagree

- The founder wants a higher-tier move: name it and give the timing; do not refuse.
- Costs are underestimated: say the lowest tier validates organic channels and the next validates paid.
- The company has more money than its stage needs: do not inflate spend. Match work to the funnel, return unused capacity, and favour things that compound over scaling.
- A tier is skipped, for example through a large round, a press breakout, or signed contracts that fund pilots: name which tier the company is effectively in.
