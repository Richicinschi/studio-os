# Customer research

## When to use

Typical wordings: "what are customers struggling with", "what do customers say about us", "analyze these interview transcripts", "mine Reddit and G2 for our category", "build personas", "find out why people churn, convert or buy", "run a voice of customer pass".

Two kinds of job, often mixed:
- Analyse material the user already holds: transcripts, survey exports, tickets, review dumps, churn notes.
- Go and gather new material from public communities and review sites.

Hand off when the research is done and the user wants the next artefact:
- Writing copy from the findings: `copywriting`.
- Fixing a page with the findings: `cro`.
- A comparison page: `competitors`.
- A churn plan built on churn findings: `churn-prevention`.
- Paid ads: `ads`. Cold outreach built on pain and trigger findings: `cold-email`.
- Turning findings into an outbound ICP: `prospecting`.
- Topics to write about: `content-strategy`. A full plan: `marketing-plan`.

## How it works

1. **Frame the job.** If a product marketing context file exists in the project (check `.agents/product-marketing.md`, or the older `product-marketing-context.md`), read it and do not re-ask what it answers. Otherwise ask first: what is the goal (messaging, personas, product gaps, churn) and what material exists. Ask for the target segment, the product and the wanted deliverable afterwards, one or two questions at a time.
2. **Pick the mode.** Material in hand means analysis. No material, or a gap, means online gathering. Say which you are doing.
3. **Collect with a log.** For every item keep: source and date, exact words, what prompted it, sentiment, one tag (pain, trigger, outcome, language, alternative, objection, competitor), and role, company size or industry hints. Quotes are copied exactly. Never write, polish or invent a quote, and mark anything paraphrased as a paraphrase. Keep only what the question needs: drop names, handles and contact details of private people unless the user has consent for them. Interviews and surveys need the participants' consent, and personal data is deleted once the findings are written up. Per-source reading order and extraction tips are in [asset-playbooks.md](customer-research/asset-playbooks.md) for owned material and [online-sources.md](customer-research/online-sources.md) for public sources.
4. **Extract six things from each item.**
   - The job, split into functional (the task), emotional (how they want to feel) and social (how they want to be seen).
   - Pains. Rank unprompted and emotionally worded ones higher.
   - Trigger events: what changed so that they started looking, such as a new hire, a missed target, an embarrassing incident, or a rival's move.
   - Desired outcomes in their own words.
   - Vocabulary. A customer's phrase beats the marketer's phrase every time.
   - Alternatives tried or weighed, including doing nothing, hiring someone, or building it in-house.
5. **Synthesise.** Cluster items into themes. Score each theme on how often it appears and how strongly it is felt. Split by segment (size, role, use case, tenure) before concluding. Choose 5 to 10 representative exact quotes per theme. Note where people say one thing and do another. Report each theme as: name, one or two sentence summary, "seen in X of Y sources", intensity, quotes with source and date, implication for messaging, product or positioning.
6. **Grade every insight before showing it.**
   - High: three or more independent sources, raised unprompted, holds across segments.
   - Medium: two sources, or prompted, or one segment only.
   - Low: one source, possible outlier, needs checking.
   Also apply the age rule (last 12 months is primary, 12 to 24 months with care, older only as background, unless a theme is stable across old and new, which makes it durable), the bias notes in [source-reliability.md](customer-research/source-reliability.md), and the floor (the skill's own guideline, a starting point and not a measured value): fewer than 5 independent points in a segment means no persona and no messaging claim for that segment.
7. **Personas, only if asked and only with enough data.** Need 5 to 10 points from one consistent segment (a starting point, not a measured value). Use [persona-template.md](customer-research/persona-template.md). No cute names, no blended "average" persona, empty fields stay empty, review every quarter.
8. **Deliver.** Offer and confirm which output is wanted before writing it: synthesis report; quote bank by theme; 1 to 3 personas; jobs map by segment; competitor intelligence (what customers say about rivals versus us); or a gap list of what is still unknown and how to find it.

## Checks before done

- Every quote is verbatim from a real item with its source and date; none were invented or tidied.
- Each insight carries a High, Medium or Low label, and no segment with under 5 data points drives a conclusion.
- Biases of the sources used are stated next to the conclusions they affect.
- Contradictions and gaps are listed, not smoothed over.
- Private people are not identifiable in the output, consent exists for interviews and surveys, and raw personal data is not kept longer than needed.
- The report is in English, with no claim that the evidence does not support.
