# Page formats

One outline per format, with the reader's intent, a URL shape and the keywords it targets.

## 1. One rival's alternative (singular)

Intent: the reader wants out of a specific tool and is looking for where to go.
URL: an alternatives folder plus the rival's name, or the rival's name with "-alternative".
Keywords: "X alternative", "alternative to X", "switch from X".

Outline:
1. The reasons people leave X, written so the reader recognises their own pain.
2. A short positioning statement: why you fit as the replacement.
3. Detailed comparison across features, service and price.
4. Who should move, and who should stay where they are.
5. How the move works.
6. Words from people who already switched.
7. One call to action.

## 2. Alternatives roundup (plural)

Intent: the reader is early and compiling a shortlist.
URL: an alternatives folder plus the rival's name with "-alternatives".
Keywords: "X alternatives", "best X alternatives", "tools like X".

Outline:
1. Common frustrations that start the search.
2. A checklist of criteria to judge any replacement.
3. The list. You go first, but include 4 to 7 real options (a starting point, not a measured value); a roundup that only lists you reads as an ad and ranks worse.
4. A summary table.
5. A short write-up per option.
6. A pick-by-situation recommendation.
7. One call to action.

## 3. You versus one rival

Intent: the reader has narrowed it to you and one other.
URL: a vs folder plus the rival's name, or a compare folder with both names.
Keywords: "you vs X" and "X vs you".

Outline:
1. Two or three sentences stating the main differences.
2. An at-a-glance table.
3. Category sections: features, pricing, support, ease of use, integrations.
4. Who you suit best.
5. Who the rival suits best, said straight.
6. Switcher quotes.
7. Migration help.
8. One call to action.

## 4. Rival versus rival

Intent: the reader compares two tools that are not yours.
URL: a compare folder with both rival names.

Outline:
1. A brief on each product.
2. Category-by-category comparison.
3. Who each one suits.
4. You, introduced as the third route.
5. A three-column table with you included.
6. One call to action.

Why bother: it collects search traffic for rival names and shows you know the market.

## Keyword map

| Format | Main phrases |
|---|---|
| Singular alternative | X alternative; alternative to X |
| Plural alternatives | X alternatives; best X alternatives |
| You vs rival | you vs X; X vs you |
| Rival vs rival | A vs B; B vs A |
