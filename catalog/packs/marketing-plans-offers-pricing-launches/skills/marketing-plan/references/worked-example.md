# Worked example: the shape of a good plan (invented scenario)

The scenario below is fictional. Names, numbers and facts are made up to show structure, depth and tone. Use it as a bar to clear, never as content to copy.

**Client:** Pawlane, a booking app for independent dog groomers plus a small branded accessories line. Archetype: B2B-leaning software with a commerce side. Funding: bootstrapped, one angel conversation open. Tier 1, no paid.

## What each section would contain (condensed)

**1. Executive summary.** Frame: turn a word-of-mouth user base into a measurable funnel before any paid spend. Bets, ranked: (1) fix the first-booking gate, because 61 percent of new groomers stop before adding a service; (2) turn the existing 14 voluntary referrers into a partner program; (3) build one search pillar around "how to run a solo grooming business". Twelve months: a funded-ready funnel, a first marketing manager, a 90-day review habit.

**2. Strategic frame.** Category: scheduling for solo pet-care professionals, not generic salon software. Voice rules: plain, practical, never "disrupt"; no guilt-based reminders.

**3. Current state.** Team of three: founder (product and sales), part-time developer, a contractor who writes monthly posts. Spend: 420 per month, all tools. Blended CAC unknown, so it leads the open decisions. Stuck: onboarding emails drafted in March, never live; blocker is a missing calendar event. Rubric total 41 of 85, strong on positioning and customer research, weak on lifecycle email, CRO and comparison pages.

**4 to 8 (summaries).**
- Acquisition: search pillar plus comparison pages against the three biggest booking apps; groomer-community posts by the founder; paid held for Tier 2.
- Activation: rebuild the first-booking flow; ship the welcome email once the calendar event exists.
- Retention: lapsed-groomer flow and a quarterly win-back; unify two churn definitions used by billing and product.
- Referral: invite the 14 referrers first; each gets a tracked link and a monthly digest.
- Revenue: pricing audit (listed 19 per month, effective 16); test an annual default.

**9. Roadmap.** Weeks 1-2 unblock: add the calendar event, fix the broken gate (founder). Weeks 3-4: pillar brief and first two pages (contractor), partner invitations (founder). Weeks 5-8: email flows live, first test of the annual default. Weeks 9-12: first partner payouts, 90-day review. Every row owned.

**10. Outlook.** Method 2, goal ARR 150K to 230K (all figures here are an invented example): 80K new ARR at 228 per customer per year is 351 customers; at an assumed CAC of 40 and 85 percent retention that is about 16.5K, plus a 15 percent experiment buffer, about 19K for the year. Pattern: linear with one planned step (a team-seat tier in Q3). Each quarter names its tier; Q3 assumes a small angel round, Tier 2 low end.

**11. Stack.** Concrete proof: in the kickoff call the founder drafted the welcome flow live through the email connector.

**12. Idea bank.** 139 mapped, with a skip list (lifetime deals, giveaways, reality-TV style stunts) each carrying a reason.

**13. Measurement.** North star: paying groomers still active at day 90 x revenue per groomer. Top open decisions: blended CAC; who owns pricing; whether the accessories line stays.

## What made it good

- Every move carried a stage tag, an owner and a named skill.
- Real numbers from the client replaced adjectives.
- Past work (the community thread that brought the first 14 referrers) was acknowledged.
- The skip list had reasons; unknowns were listed, not hidden.
- The summary could be forwarded to the angel unchanged.
