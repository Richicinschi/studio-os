# Headline formulas

Pick the family that fits the strongest fact you have, then fill the blanks with concrete detail. Every example is a pattern, not a claim to reuse.

## Outcome first
- Get {result} without {pain}. Example: "Plan your week without juggling five calendars."
- Get {result} by {mechanism}. Example: "Win back lapsed clients by spotting who opened your last quote."
- Turn {input} into {result}.
- {Result} in {timeframe}. Use a timeframe only if it is true and provable.

## Pain first
- Never {bad event} again.
- A question that names the pain: "Tired of rewriting the same status update?"
- Stop {pain}. Start {relief}.

## Audience first
- {Product type} for {audience}.
- {Product type} for {audience} to {use}.
- You do not need {skill or resource} to {result}.

## Difference first
- The {unusual route} way to {result}.
- The {category} that {differentiator}. Example: "The inbox that sorts itself."

## Proof first
- {Number} {people} use {product} to {result}. Only with a real, current number.
- A plain statement of the key benefit: "Keep every shift swap in one place."

## Other shapes
- The simple way to {result}.
- Finally, {category} that {benefit}.
- Get {benefit} from your existing {thing}.
- {Verb} your {thing} like {admired example}.
- What if you could {result}?
- Everything you need to {result}.
- The {adjective} {category} built for {audience}.

## Working method
1. Write six headlines from at least three families.
2. Cut any with a buzzword or an unprovable claim.
3. Test each against the visitor's source: does it match the ad or link they clicked?
4. Keep the top two or three as options and say why.
