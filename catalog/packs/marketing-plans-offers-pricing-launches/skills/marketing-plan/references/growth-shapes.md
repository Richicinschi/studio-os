# Growth shapes

Use this at step 6 (pick the lens) and step 8 (growth phase is one of the nine tailoring facts). It tells you what the 12-month outlook may assume and which target style fits.

## What the plan must decide

1. The revenue phase and what limits it now.
2. Middle phase: the monthly revenue that lets founders work full time.
3. Later phase: whether growth is speeding up.
4. The growth pattern: steady monthly additions, or flat stretches ended by deliberate jumps, and what causes each jump.
5. Each channel's maturity, and which channel to start next.
6. Where the product and the market need to grow.
7. The target style and the headline measure for the phase.

## Revenue is lumpy

Recurring revenue grows in overlapping rises and flat spells. The outlook must say so, and must never imply the same high percentage gain every month. Judge progress by absolute monthly additions. Adding 20K a month for 24 months is 480K of new recurring revenue, which is strong even though the percentage gain shrinks each month.

## Phases by ARR

These boundaries are judgement, not measured data; move them if the client's case says otherwise.

- Below about 10K ARR: each customer is hard to win, and positioning, price and channel may all be wrong. The limit is finding one channel that works, and founders need runway, often from outside income.
- About 10K to 100K: revenue shows the idea works but cannot yet pay a team. Many companies fail here by running out of cash just as traction shows. The limit is funding the people. Work out the monthly revenue that lets the founders go full time, from their living costs, and plan to reach it. Activation work usually pays off most in this stretch.
- About 100K to 1M: later steps often come faster than early ones, because referrals and happy early customers do some of the selling. Compare how long the first 100K took with how long the step from 100K to 200K is taking; a shorter second span means growth is speeding up. The limit is raising the ceiling of the channel that got you here. Retention and referral work usually matters most.
- Above 1M: the phases stop applying. Name the limit from the funnel diagnosis in funnel-stages.md.

Headline measure: new signups early; net revenue retention or expansion later. This is judgement; give your reasoning.

## Two patterns

- Steady additions: a working channel adds a predictable amount each month. This suits hiring, roadmap and expansion planning. Plan these in the funnel-stage sections.
- Flat stretches with jumps: a jump comes from a new segment, a new product line or tier, or a channel that starts compounding. Build the next jump while still on the current level instead of waiting for it. Schedule jumps in the 12-month section, and name the deliberate action behind each.

## Three overlapping curves

Growth lasts when channels, product and market each have a curve and the curves are staggered.

Channels, with their usual behaviour:
- Search: slow to mature, then pays for years. Judge it from your own rankings and traffic.
- Paid: quick, but loses efficiency as spend rises.
- Content: slow start, then compounds.
- Partnerships: irregular, sometimes large.
- Outbound: predictable and expensive per customer, capped by team time.
- PR: creates spikes and awareness more than conversions.

Label each live channel early, mature or flattening. Start the next channel before the current one levels off, and give the reason.

Product: the first market saturates, and more of the same does not help. Add use cases, adjacent offers or a new segment.

Market: each segment has a ceiling, whether that is small to large customers, one vertical to another, or home country to abroad. Time the move into the next segment early, before the current one stops growing.

Show the sequence: one curve maturing, another giving quick wins, a third being prepared.

## Habits for the planner

- Name the limiting constraint in every outlook.
- A flat period usually means one curve has levelled off before the next has lifted, so prepare the next move for that gap.
- Do not let a falling growth percentage be read as failure when monthly additions hold.

## Target style

Venture-backed companies above 1M ARR can anchor on their own history (budget-math.md). Bootstrapped or profit-focused companies use linear or step targets.
