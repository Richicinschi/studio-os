# Anatomy of a complete offer

Six parts. A missing or weak part is usually why an offer stalls.

| # | Part | Question it answers | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core deliverable | What do they get? | Vague, or a list of features with no outcome |
| 2 | Bonus stack | What removes my remaining doubts? | None, padded, or inflated |
| 3 | Guarantee | What if it does not work? | Missing, generic, or reckless |
| 4 | Scarcity or urgency | Why now? | Missing, or fake |
| 5 | Name | What do I call this? | Generic, jargon, or absent |
| 6 | Price and payment structure | What do I pay, and how? | One number, one way to pay |

## 1. Core deliverable

Frame it as the outcome, then list the features as support.

- Feature framing: "5 modules, 20 lessons, a forum."
- Outcome framing: "A booking funnel that fills your diary, built alongside you over 8 weeks."

Write the scope in three lists: included, not included, optional. Buyers pay for clarity.

```
Included:
- 6-week program, weekly group call (recorded)
- Shared channel with a response inside one working day
- 8 fill-in templates
- One 60-minute review of your own material

Not included:
- Extra one-to-one calls
- Doing the work for you (we coach, you execute)
- Software licences
```

Depth depends on the buyer. A sophisticated buyer wants method and scope. Someone new to the category wants the outcome and proof first.

## 2. Bonus stack

Bonuses lift perceived value, soften risk, and each can answer one specific objection. Keep to a few, keep values honest. Full method in bonuses.md.

## 3. Guarantee

It lifts belief and lowers the buyer's risk. The wrong type does damage: over-promising draws refund hunters, a vague "100% guaranteed" looks unenforceable, and none at all can read as doubt. Full method in guarantees.md.

## 4. Scarcity or urgency

Scarcity limits quantity; urgency limits time. Use it only when the constraint is real. See scarcity.md.

## 5. Name

A named offer can be repeated, stands apart from a generic service, and supports a higher price than the same work sold as plain hours.

Patterns that work:
- Outcome name: "The 6-Week Booking Fix"
- Method name: "The Fill-the-Diary Routine"
- Identity name: "Studio Owners' Workshop"
- Shape name: "Weekend Intensive"

Patterns that fail: generic labels ("Marketing Program"), internal codes ("Plan B Standard"), get-rich-quick phrasing, and pun piles.

Test: could a buyer text a friend, "I joined the [name], it costs X and gets you [one-line result]"? If not, rename.

## 6. Price and payment structure

Price is a comparison with: the outcome, the next best alternative, the cost of doing nothing, and other items in your catalogue. You can change how the price feels without changing the number: make the cost of doing nothing vivid, show a higher-priced alternative, or present a premium tier first.

Payment structure is a separate lever.

| Structure | Fits | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Pay in full | High-trust buyers, smaller tickets | Smallest buyer pool |
| Two to four installments | Mid-size tickets, hesitant buyers | Missed payments |
| Monthly subscription | Software, ongoing service | Churn |
| Pay after results | Confident delivery, expert buyers | Slow cash, hard attribution |
| Deposit plus balance on delivery | Milestone work | Risk on the balance |
| Free trial into paid | Low-friction software and products | Drop-off at conversion |

Often the better move is a payment plan, not a lower price. Down-sells (a smaller, cheaper version) and upsells (a faster or done-for-you version) also belong here: decide them now, so a "no" to the main offer has somewhere to go.

## Assembled example: a bookkeeping service

| Part | Weak | Stronger |
|---|---|---|
| Core | "Bookkeeping help" | "Your books closed monthly by day 5, with a one-page cash summary" |
| Bonuses | None | A year-end checklist; a receipt-capture setup call; a shared questions thread |
| Guarantee | None | "If month one's close is not delivered by day 5, month one is free" |
| Scarcity | None | "I take 6 new clients per quarter; the next opening is on a stated date" |
| Name | "Bookkeeping" | "The Day-5 Close" |
| Price | "Monthly fee" | Monthly fee, with a quarterly prepay option |

Same work, different offer. Many price complaints turn out to be one of the six parts being absent.
