# Context sections: what to ask for

Work through these in order. Skip any that do not apply, and say so.

## 1. Product
- One sentence on what it is.
- Two or three sentences on what it does.
- Category: the shelf a buyer looks on when searching.
- Type: software subscription, marketplace, online shop, service, and so on.
- Business model and price points.

## 2. Audience
- Who buys: industry, company size, growth stage (or, for consumers, life situation).
- Who decides: job titles and teams.
- The main problem solved.
- Two or three jobs the customer hires the product to do.
- Concrete situations where it gets used.

## 3. Buying roles (business sales only)
When several people take part in a purchase, list each role: end user, internal champion, final approver, budget holder, technical adviser. For each, note what they care about, their own difficulty, and the value promised to them. Consumer products skip this.

## 4. Pain
- The difficulty before they found you.
- Why today's options disappoint.
- The cost: hours, money, missed chances.
- The feeling underneath: worry, fear, doubt.

## 5. Competition
Sort rivals into three kinds and note how each lets the buyer down.
- Head to head: same answer to the same problem.
- Substitute product: a different answer to the same problem.
- Different philosophy: a rival approach, such as doing it by hand or hiring a person.

## 6. Differentiation
- What you can do that others cannot.
- How your method differs.
- Why that difference helps the buyer.
- The reason people pick you.

## 7. Objections and misfits
- The three doubts raised most often in sales talks, and an honest reply to each.
- The kind of customer who should not buy.

## 8. Switching forces
- Push: what annoys them about the current way.
- Pull: what draws them to you.
- Habit: what keeps them where they are.
- Anxiety: what scares them about changing.

## 9. Customer language
- How they describe the problem, word for word.
- How they describe your product, word for word.
- Terms to use and terms to avoid.
- A glossary of product-specific vocabulary.

## 10. Voice
- Tone (formal, relaxed, playful).
- Style (direct, chatty, technical).
- Three to five adjectives for the brand's character.

## 11. Proof
- Figures the user can back up.
- Named customers or logos they have permission to show.
- Short testimonial excerpts that really exist.
- Main value themes, each with its evidence.

## 12. Goals
- The main business aim.
- The one action you want visitors to take.
- Current numbers, if known.
