# Personalization

Research-based detail can lift replies, but only when it relates to the problem you solve. A detail that has no link to your offer is an attention trick, and readers feel the difference.

## Four levels

1. Merge fields (name, company, title). Expected by everyone, so it adds little on its own.
2. Segment. Pains and trends common to an industry. Scales through tight segmenting.
   Example: "Most dental groups wrestle with empty slots, which quietly eats margin."
3. Role. What this job title struggles with.
   Example: "As practice manager you juggle staff rotas and patient calls, usually with no extra help."
4. Individual. A timely observation about this person or company, tied to your offer.
   Example: "Your careers page lists a second hygienist, so volume is clearly rising. Confirmation work tends to jump at that point."

## Signals to look for

| Signal | Where | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| A recent investment round | investor announcements, press | growth creates a specific strain |
| Open roles | careers page, job boards | the hire shows where effort is going |
| Tools in use | stack lookup sites, browser extensions | "teams on that tool often hit a ceiling at X" |
| Public posts and job changes | professional network | refer to a concrete point they made |
| Company news | news search, press releases | mergers, openings, launches |
| Talks and podcasts | video and audio search | quote one real idea |
| Site changes | manual look | a new pricing or booking page |

## A fast routine

1. List the signals you can find most easily (a starting point: five about companies and five about people), ranked by relevance to your offer.
2. Fix a three-part skeleton: observation tied to a problem, the problem you solve, one sentence of solution plus a light ask.
3. Write one ready-made opening per signal type, each ending with a natural bridge into the problem. The rest of the mail stays constant, so each message takes minutes.

## Four kinds of profile data

- Demographic: background, profession.
- Technographic: tools used.
- Firmographic: size, stage, industry, funding.
- Psychographic: what they care about and believe.

## Strong openers

- Trigger event: the funding, hire or launch, then the strain it usually brings.
- Observation: a specific point from something they published, then how it connects to the problem.
- Peer insight: what people in their role commonly report, then asking whether it matches their case.

## What feels fake

- Phrases that sound like automation ("I came across your profile").
- A friendly aside unrelated to the pitch (school, hometown) followed by a sales turn.
- Details so personal they feel like surveillance.
- Praise with nothing specific behind it.

## The so-what test

Read the first line as the recipient and ask "so what?" If nothing answers, rewrite it or cut it. Also drop the opener and reread: if the mail still works unchanged, the opener was decoration.

Only use information a person has made public in a professional setting, and never guess at private traits.
