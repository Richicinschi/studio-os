# Five launch stages

A launch is a ladder. Each rung widens the audience and tests something different. Do not skip rungs for a product nobody outside the team has used.

| Stage | Who sees it | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Internal | hand-picked friendly people | does the core idea work at all |
| Alpha | invited individuals, public landing page | will strangers sign up and use it |
| Beta | the early-access list plus trusted voices | what breaks and what do people say |
| Early access | a growing share of the list | does it hold up at scale |
| Full launch | everyone | will they pay |

The details of each rung follow.

## 1. Internal

Goal: confirm the core idea works for friendly people.
- Recruit a few people one by one and let them use it free.
- Note usability gaps and missing features.
- The prototype must be demonstrable; it need not be production quality.

## 2. Alpha

Goal: first outside validation and the start of a waiting list.
- Publish a landing page with an early-access signup form.
- Say publicly that the product exists.
- Invite people individually to try it.
- The minimum product should run in production, even if it still changes weekly.

## 3. Beta

Goal: build buzz and improve with wider feedback.
- Work through the early-access list; some seats free, some paid.
- Begin teasing the problem you solve.
- Ask friends, advisors and respected voices to try it and talk about it.

Useful extras: a "coming soon" page or waitlist, a beta badge in the app navigation, invitation emails, and a settings switch that turns on experimental features.

## 4. Early access

Goal: prove it holds at larger scale and prepare the full release.
- Release glimpses: screenshots, short feature clips, demos.
- Collect numbers (usage) and words (feedback).
- Interview engaged users, with a reward such as account credit.
- Optionally run a "how disappointed would you be" survey to sharpen the message.

Two ways to widen access:
- Staged: invite in batches, around 5 to 10 percent of the list each time (a starting point, not a measured value).
- Open: invite everyone at once but keep the "early access" label.

## 5. Full launch

Goal: maximum visibility and conversion to paying users.
- Open self-serve signup.
- Start charging, if you have not already (pricing decisions go through the owner).
- Announce general availability everywhere at once.

Touchpoints to line up: emails to customers, in-app pop-ups or a short tour, a banner on the site pointing to launch material, a "New" badge in navigation, a blog post, social posts on each active network, and directories such as Product Hunt, BetaList or Hacker News. Posting to any of these is an outward act that waits for the owner's word.
