# Subject lines

The subject has one job: get the mail opened. The pattern to start from is short, plain and ordinary-looking. Everything here is a starting point to test against your own results, not a measured rule.

## Length

- Two to four words is the skill's starting point. Test two words against four and compare replies, not only opens.
- Phones cut long subjects off, so look at how yours displays on a phone before sending.

## Look like an internal note

Subjects that resemble a colleague's message tend to read as work mail rather than sales mail, because people sort mail into "sales" or "work" before opening. Good shapes: "chair utilization", "front desk hours", "second site", "march schedule", "weekend cover", "new hygienist".

## Lowercase

All-lowercase is the skill's default for cold mail: it reads as personal and unpolished in a good way. Test it against sentence case on your own list.

## Context beats a name

Personalised subjects can help, but a first name in the subject signals a merge and can cost replies. Use context instead: the pain, a rival, a trigger event, a shared interest.

## Questions

Published advice conflicts. The consistent reading is that a specific question works ("need cover for weekends?") and a generic one fails ("quick question?"). Default to a plain statement.

## Avoid

| Pattern | Why |
|---|---|
| Sales words such as increase, boost, ROI | reads as a pitch and invites filters |
| Urgency (ASAP, urgent) | reads as pressure |
| Repeated punctuation | reads as spam |
| Numbers and percentages | can read as a promotion |
| Emoji | looks unprofessional for B2B |
| Pitching the product | the reader decides before opening |
| Blank subject | reads as careless |
| Spam words (free, guarantee, act now) | filtering risk |

Never fake a reply or forward prefix. Test any of these on a small split before dropping a rule.

## Senior readers

Executives get a heavy volume of mail and decide in seconds. What works: very short, human, understated ("an update", "thank you", a project name, a trigger event). Anything that smells like a pitch is dropped.
