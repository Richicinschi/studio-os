# Answering reporters' calls for sources

Reporters post "I need an expert on X". You reply and sometimes get quoted. It is the cheapest PR route, and only worth doing if you take it seriously. Every reply is a draft for the owner to approve before it goes anywhere.

## Platforms

| Platform | What it is |
|---|---|
| HARO (Help A Reporter Out) | Email digests of reporter queries. The Cision-run service and its Connectively successor were closed in December 2024; the HARO name was then sold to Featured and the digests restarted in 2025. Confirm the current owner and format on the service's own site |
| Qwoted | Web app with reporter requests |
| Featured | Expert profiles plus a request feed; reported to have absorbed the Terkel roundup service |
| Help A B2B Writer | Email list of B2B queries; reported to have changed hands and possibly name, so check it still exists |
| SourceBottle | Started in Australia, takes queries from several countries |
| JournoRequests | A social account that collects requests |
| #JournoRequest | A live hashtag |

Platforms in this niche get sold, renamed and shut down often (the HARO line did all three within about two years), so check each one's current status, ownership, plans and prices on its own site before relying on it, and judge quality by your own log (below).

Starter set: HARO, Qwoted, one B2B list that still runs, plus the hashtag. Pick 2 or 3, not all of them. Signing up to a platform or paying for a tier needs the owner's word first, and you state the cost.

## Set-up before the first reply

- Press page is current.
- A one-line credential is written.
- A high-resolution headshot sits at a public link.
- A handful of specific stories or data points are ready (3 to 5 is a starting point).
- Two or three platforms are chosen.
- A short daily slot is blocked (20 minutes is a starting point).
- A tracker exists.

## Daily triage

**Filter (5 minutes).** Drop a request if you have no direct experience or data, if its outlet is not read by your audience, if you cannot make the deadline, or if you cannot give a complete, named, on-the-record answer. Count how many keepers you get per day for the first two weeks; that is your own baseline.

**Reply (note your own time per reply in the log).** Read the request three times to find the real angle. Look up the reporter's beat. Write a custom reply and send it before the deadline; earlier is better.

**Log.** Date, platform, reporter and outlet, topic, whether you replied, outcome (no answer, passed, quoted, linked). After a few dozen replies (a starting point, not a measured value) you can see which topics and platforms pay off.

## Reply template (under 200 words)

```
Hi <name>,

Quick answer to your request on <topic>.

<One sentence of concrete credential.>

The main point on <topic>: <your point in two sentences>.

<One specific example, story or figure. This is the part that gets quoted.>

<Optional: an angle that differs from the usual answers.>

You are welcome to quote me directly or ask for more detail.
Attribution: <name>, <title>, <company>
Contact: <email and phone>
```

## What gets chosen

- **Quotable detail.** Weak: "firms should focus on customer experience." Strong: a precise test result with its count and its effect, taken from real work.
- **Concrete credential.** "Ran growth for three B2B tools" beats "marketing expert".
- **Story over advice.** A short account of what went wrong and what changed beats "track the right metrics".
- **Their workflow.** Include the attribution, several quotable lines to choose from, a link to a headshot (not an attachment) and a one-line company description.
- **Time.** Send early. Log the hours between the request and your reply, and compare that with which replies got quoted. Until you have that log, treat every deadline as a day earlier than stated (a starting point).

Use only real results from the user's own work.

## What sinks a reply

- Pitching the product when expert comment was asked for.
- Generic advice anyone could give.
- Several people from one company answering the same request.
- Hiring a firm to mass-answer.
- Demanding a backlink.
- Missing the deadline by a day or more.
- A long bio before the answer.
- Asking to see the piece before it runs.
- Asking what other experts said.

## What to expect

Measure your own baseline instead of trusting a promise. Over 90 days, log hours spent per week, replies sent, and pieces that quoted you. Compare custom replies with template replies and with any firm you try, then keep the route with the most quotes per hour.

A quote in a top outlet gives a backlink, sales proof, AI-citation chance and credibility. In a small outlet it often gives only a nofollow link.

Decision rule: if your own log shows quotes per hour that justify the time, keep going; if you can only spare very little time, put it into proactive pitching.
