## When to use

The user already has text and wants it better, not written from scratch. Typical wordings:
- Edit and review: "edit this copy", "review my copy", "copy feedback", "proofread", "copy sweep".
- Polish: "polish this", "make this better", "tighten this up", "too wordy", "clean up this text", "this reads awkwardly".
- Message: "sharpen the messaging".
- Refresh: "refresh this content", "update this page", "this content is outdated", "content audit".

Neighbours and where each takes over:
- Writing a page, email or ad from nothing: the copywriting skill. Come back here once a first draft exists.
- Structure, layout or strategy of the whole page: the cro skill.
- Why a given change persuades: marketing-psychology.
- Testing two versions against each other: ab-testing.

## How it works

1. **Load context.** If a product marketing context file exists (look in `.agents/`, then `.claude/`, then the older `product-marketing-context.md` name), read it first. Take the brand voice and the customers' own words from it.
2. **Ask what you do not know.** What is the copy for (awareness, conversion or retention)? What should the reader do next? Any known problems? What proof exists (quotes, numbers, logos)? Is this a first edit or a refresh of something live? Read the whole text once without touching it.
3. **Run seven focused passes, in this order:** clarity, voice and tone, so what, prove it, specificity, heightened emotion, zero risk. Each pass looks at one thing only. Details and the questions for each pass are in [sweeps](copy-editing/references/sweeps.md).
4. **Loop back after every pass.** When pass N is done, re-read through passes N-1 down to clarity to check the new edits broke nothing. After pass 7 make one full return trip through all seven.
5. **Score high-stakes copy with a panel.** Launch copy, pricing pages and busy landing pages get a multi-persona score on top of the passes. Rules, panels and rubric: [panel scoring](copy-editing/references/panel-scoring.md).
6. **Work as an editor, not a ghostwriter.** For each pass show what you found and why it matters, propose a concrete replacement, and let the author decide. Ask for the revised text, re-check earlier passes on it, and repeat until a full round finds nothing new. Keep the author's voice and the core message. Every edit needs a reason.
7. **Refresh instead of edit when the page has aged.** Falling traffic, stats roughly a year old or more, a changed product or newer competitor pages call for a refresh. Passes, the refresh-or-rewrite matrix and review cadence: [content refresh](copy-editing/references/content-refresh.md).

For a quick job, skip the seven passes and use [quick-pass checks](copy-editing/references/quick-pass.md) (words, sentences, paragraphs) and the word list in [plain words](copy-editing/references/plain-words.md). Typical text problems with their fixes (feature walls, corporate speak, buried calls to action, mixed audiences) are in [common problems](copy-editing/references/common-problems.md).

## Checks before done

Run the full [checklist](copy-editing/references/checklist.md). The short version:
- The goal, audience and desired action are known.
- Each of the seven passes was done, plus the return trip.
- The core message survived every edit, and no sentence got longer without a reason.
- No unearned claim remains: every "best" or "trusted by" has proof or has been softened.
- No typos, formatting is consistent, links work.
- Copy the user will send or publish was handed back for their approval, not published by you.
