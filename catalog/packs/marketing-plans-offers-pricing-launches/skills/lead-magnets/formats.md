# Building each format

Short build notes for the formats in the main skill. Page counts, lengths and timings below are starting points, not measured values.

## Ebook or guide
Best for authority and early-stage readers. Order: cover, contents, an intro that names the problem, 3-7 chapters with one idea each, a recap, then a closing step toward the product. Aim for 10-25 pages; shorter is fine when dense. Use charts, screenshots and call-out boxes, and close each chapter with one takeaway. Density beats length. Any layout tool or a word processor exported to PDF is enough.

## Checklist
Best for processes and quick wins. Title it "[N] checks for [topic]". One to two pages, tick boxes, items ordered by workflow or priority, grouped if there are more than ten, one or two sentences each, starting with a verb. Make it print cleanly.

## Cheat sheet
One page, two at most, grouped by task, dense but scannable, with bold on the items readers need most. Built for lookup: tables, columns, formulas, shortcuts, snippets. Suits desk printing.

## Templates
- Spreadsheet: a "how to use" tab, sample data filled in, dropdowns through data validation, conditional formatting for cues, formula cells locked and input cells open, and a make-a-copy link.
- Notion: share a duplicate link, add a short start page, include example content, use the database views, keep it plain.
- Document: supply Google Doc, Word and PDF versions, mark blanks in square brackets, put instructions in a second color.
Rule: usable within 5 minutes of opening it.

## Swipe file
15-50 curated examples sorted by use. For each: the example itself, 2-3 notes on why it works, and 1-2 sentences on adapting it. Cut hard, add your own analysis, refresh now and then, and name the original source of every example.

## Mini-courses
- Email: 3-5 messages over 5-7 days, one concept each, built as teach, show, practice, getting harder. The last one recaps and points to a next step.
- Video: 3-5 clips of 5-15 minutes, hosted unlisted, sent through a drip, with a worksheet per lesson. More personal, more work.
Send every 1-2 days; do not stretch the gaps too wide or squeeze them too tight. Each lesson must be useful alone.

## Quiz or assessment
5-10 multiple-choice questions (about 7 is right), no free text, insightful rather than obvious, with a progress counter. 3-5 result types, each with a name, a description and tailored advice, and a result worth sharing. Let people take the quiz openly and ask for the email only to show the result. Follow-up emails can differ by result. Building the quiz itself: free-tools.

## Webinar or workshop
- Live: 30-45 minutes of teaching plus 15 of questions. Flow: hook, three teaching points, a demo, a call to action. Promote 1-2 weeks ahead, send three reminders (confirmation, day before, one hour before), record it.
- Evergreen: recorded, edited tighter, always on, behind an email registration with an automatic follow-up.
Send replay, summary and next step within 24 hours. A pure pitch damages trust.
