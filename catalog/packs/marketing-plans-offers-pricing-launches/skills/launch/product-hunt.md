# Product Hunt and similar directories

A directory launch can bring a burst of early adopters, but it rewards preparation, not luck.

## What you get and what it costs

Gains:
- Reach into a technical early-adopter crowd.
- A credibility badge if you place near the top.
- Possible press mentions and links from other sites.

Costs:
- Ranking is hard because many teams launch the same day.
- Traffic spikes fade fast.
- Heavy preparation beforehand, plus a full day of attention from the team.

If the team cannot give the day, skip it and say so in the plan.

## The three windows

Weeks before:
1. Make the listing itself the first deliverable: one clear tagline, clean images, a short demo video. Then read a handful of earlier launches in your category and compare their pages with yours.
2. Spend the lead time in the communities where your audience reads, and give help before you ask for anything. Build ties with people who might back you and with the sites and groups they follow.
3. Decide who on the team answers what, and block their calendars for the whole day.

The day itself (treat it as an all-day event):
1. Reply to each comment promptly and start discussions of your own; go deep on questions.
2. Tell your existing audience through the channels they already use. Check the directory's current rules on asking for votes before you word that message.
3. Point visitors at a page on your own site that captures their email.

Afterwards:
1. Write to everyone who engaged, and move the visitors onto your list.
2. Keep publishing follow-up content while interest lasts.

## Worked walk-through (invented)

A small team launches a habit-tracking app. Four weeks ahead they comment helpfully in two maker forums and ask three newsletter authors for feedback. They rewrite the tagline five times and film a 45-second demo. On the day one person answers comments while another watches signups, and the landing page offers a free month for an email. Next morning they send a thank-you note to everyone who commented and add the new subscribers to the onboarding series. The point of the example is the sequence, not the result: no outcome numbers are promised.
