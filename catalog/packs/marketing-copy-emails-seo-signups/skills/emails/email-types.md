# Lifecycle and campaign email types

Use as a menu when deciding what a program is missing, then run the audit list at the end. Every count, delay and threshold below is the skill's own starting point, not a measured value: tune it against your own data.

## Onboarding

- **New users series.** Trigger: free signup or trial start. Goal: reach the first moment of value. Usually 5-7 emails over 15 days: welcome with one next step, quick win on day 2, feature spotlight on day 4, success story on day 6, check-in and offer of help on day 8, advanced tip on day 11, upgrade or next milestone on day 15. Watch activation rate and feature adoption.
- **New customers series.** Trigger: conversion to paid. Goal: confirm the decision and cut early churn. 3-5 emails over 15 days: thanks and what happens next, setup checklist on day 3, tips for paid features on day 6, a similar customer's story on day 9, check-in with support resources on day 15. They have already committed, so reinforce and expand; do not sell again.
- **Key step reminder.** Trigger: a critical setup step is still undone after a set time (for example no data source linked after 72 hours, no second project created after 4 days, no avatar or name set after 12 hours). One email or a mini-sequence of 2-3. Remind them what they started, why the step matters, link straight to it, offer help.
- **Invite email.** Trigger: an existing user invites a colleague; the recipient is the invitee. Day 0 invitation, day 3 reminder, day 6 final reminder. Name the inviter, say what they are joining, one button to accept.

## Retention

- **Upgrade to paid.** Trigger by time (trial day 9, 11, 13), by behavior (hit a limit, tried a premium feature) or by engagement (very active free user). 3-5 emails: summary of what they achieved, what they are missing, who else upgraded, urgency, last chance with an easy path.
- **Upgrade to a higher plan.** Trigger: near a limit (for example three quarters of seats or most of the storage; pick your own threshold) or touching a higher-tier feature. Frame it as growth, show what the next tier unlocks, compare value with cost, make the move easy.
- **Ask for a review.** Trigger: a milestone such as day 45, 90 or 120, a result, a solved ticket, a renewal. Send after good moments, never right after billing trouble or bugs. Thank them, cite the specific value, say why reviews help others, link directly. Keep it short.
- **Proactive support.** Trigger: signs of struggle, such as usage dropping week over week, repeated failed actions, repeated help-page views, stalling on one step. Warm tone, specific ("I noticed ..." when data allows), real help offered by a person, no pitch.
- **Usage report.** Weekly, monthly or quarterly. Metrics, comparison with the last period, achievements, suggestions, a light link. Aim to make them feel the value they received.
- **NPS survey.** Twice a year or after a milestone. Ask only the score first, then a follow-up form. Sender is a real person. Say how feedback gets used. Follow-up by score: 9-10 thank and ask for a review or referral; 7-8 ask what would make it a 10; 0-6 personal outreach (the standard NPS bands).
- **Referral.** Trigger: milestone, promoter score or campaign; good after a win or renewal. Recall their success, explain the offer plainly, give a unique link, show the benefit to both sides.

## Billing

- **Switch to annual.** Around the monthly renewal, at year end, after 4-8 loyal months, or alongside a price rise. Show exact savings, any extras, and a one-click switch.
- **Failed payment recovery.** 3-4 emails: day 0 friendly notice with an update link, day 2 reminder that service may pause, day 6 urgent suspension warning, day 12-15 final notice of what will be lost. Assume an accident, be direct without guilt, one button, explain the consequence. Track recovery rate and time to recovery.
- **Cancellation survey.** Sent at once on cancel. In-app asking gets better completion; email is the fallback; high-value accounts get personal outreach. Ask the main reason, what could be better, whether anything would change their mind, whether you can help them move on. Use the answer to offer a fitting save: discount, pause, downgrade or training.
- **Renewal reminder.** 21 or 45 days before. Date and amount, what is included, how to change payment or plan, any pricing or feature changes, optionally an upsell. Needed for annual plans and large contracts.

## Usage

- **Periodic summary.** Daily for very high-engagement products, weekly for activity and suggestions, monthly for a fuller report and return on investment. Content must reflect real use: an empty report is worse than none.
- **Milestones and key events.** One email per event: first action done, the 25th or 250th item, goal reached, team milestone, a streak. Celebrate, state the achievement, give context, point to the next milestone.

## Win-back

- **Expired trials.** 3-4 emails over about 5 weeks: day 2 what they are missing, day 9 ask what held them back, day 20 incentive such as a discount or extended trial, day 35 a last open-door note. Vary by engagement: heavy users need friction removed, light users a fresh start and more help, silent users a question and a demo offer.
- **Cancelled customers.** Day 45 what is new, day 90 the fix for the common reason they left, day 135 a special offer to return. No guilt, tailor to their stated reason, make coming back simple.

## Campaigns

- **Newsletter or monthly roundup.** Product tips, customer stories, education, company news, industry views. Same day and time each cycle, scannable, one focus for the call to action. Some unsubscribes keep the list healthy.
- **Seasonal promotion.** Burst of 2-3 emails around a calendar moment (spring launch, quarter end, summer sale, autumn restart, industry seasons): announcement, mid-promotion reminder, last-hours notice.
- **Product update.** One email per major release: what changed, why it matters, how to use it with a direct link, credit to those who asked. Target by relevance, and consider power users first.
- **Industry news roundup.** Weekly or monthly curated links with your commentary, what it means for readers and how your product helps. Best for B2B audiences who follow their sector.
- **Pricing change.** Announce 45-75 days ahead, remind at 21 days, final notice at 10. Be plain, explain why, grandfather existing rates when possible, offer options such as annual lock-in or a downgrade. Honest notice protects trust.

## Audit list

Tick what exists today.
- Onboarding: new users series, new customers series, step reminders, invite sequence.
- Retention: upgrade to paid, plan-upgrade triggers, review ask (well timed), proactive support, usage reports, NPS, referral.
- Billing: annual switch, failed payment recovery, cancellation survey, renewal reminders.
- Usage: periodic summaries, key event notices, milestone celebrations.
- Win-back: expired trial flow, cancelled customer flow.
- Campaigns: newsletter, seasonal calendar, product update announcements, pricing communications.
