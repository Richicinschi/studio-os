# Thinking models for strategy

Use these before tactics. They decide which problem is worth solving.

- **First principles.** Strip a plan down to facts, asking "why" up to five times. A yoga studio that wants a podcast because rivals have one should ask what the podcast is for. The answer may be booking trial classes, and a free class link in the footer could do that faster.
- **Jobs to be done.** Customers hire a product for a result. A parent buying a night light wants a child who stays in bed. Sell the quiet evening, not the lumen count.
- **Circle of competence.** Work the channels where you already have skill or an unfair advantage. A photographer's shop should lead with visuals, not forced written threads. Hire help for anything outside the circle.
- **Inversion.** Instead of asking how to win, write down what would guarantee a failed launch (an unclear headline, the wrong audience, a checkout that times out) and take each item off the list before anything else.
- **Occam's razor.** A drop in sign-ups most often has a dull cause. Test the form, page speed and the security certificate before you blame attribution, seasonality or the market.
- **Pareto (80/20).** A few channels, customers and pieces of content usually carry most results. Rank them, protect the top slice, and trim the tail.
- **Local vs global optimum.** A better button colour is a local gain. If your buyers never visit the site because they live in a chat app, the global fix is a different place to meet them. Zoom out before you tune.
- **Theory of constraints.** One step limits the whole flow. If 4 in 10 visitors buy but only 30 visitors arrive per week, conversion work is wasted; fix reach.
- **Opportunity cost.** Every hour on a weak channel is an hour not spent on a strong one. Compare against the best alternative, not against doing nothing.
- **Diminishing returns.** The twelfth article on a single topic adds less than the second did. Once each extra hour buys less, send effort to a new channel or format.
- **Second-order thinking.** Follow an action one step past its first result. A recurring promotion raises this week's takings and also trains customers to hold off until the next one.
- **Map is not the territory.** Personas and dashboards are summaries. Talk to a handful of real customers each month so the model stays honest.
- **Probabilistic thinking.** Give a campaign a rough chance of working (say 3 in 10) and plan what you do in the other 7. Never stake the whole budget on one bet.
- **Barbell strategy.** Put most money in proven work and a small slice in bold experiments. Skip the moderate-risk, moderate-reward middle where nothing is learned.

Ethical lens for this group: use them to focus effort, not to justify cutting corners on honesty.
