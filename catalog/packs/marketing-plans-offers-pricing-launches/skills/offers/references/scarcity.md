# Scarcity and urgency, only the real kind

Rule: show only real constraints, or none. If a limit is invented, remove it from the page.

## Two levers

| Lever | Limits | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Scarcity | How many | seats, units, capacity, batch |
| Urgency | By when | enrollment close, season, bonus end, price rise |

Both turn "I will think about it" into a decision. They differ in what enforces the limit.

## Honest formats

- Capacity: you can serve N clients at once. "I take three new clients a quarter; the next opening is on a stated date." Fits services and coaching.
- Cohort: a group starts on a date and the door shuts before it. "The autumn group starts on the first Monday of October; enrollment closes the Friday before." Fits courses and programs.
- Founding price: early buyers pay less, and the price actually rises afterwards. If it never rises, it was a lie.
- Inventory: a fixed run, no reprint. Fits physical goods and numbered digital editions.
- Season: demand or capacity follows the calendar, for example a filing service that stops taking new clients after a stated spring date.
- Bonus end: the core stays the same, an extra goes away on a date. Remove it on that date.
- Scheduled price rise: announce the date and the new price, and let early buyers keep the old price; the buyer can check it later.

## Fake patterns to remove

- A countdown that resets itself.
- "Only a few left" on something with no stock limit, or a number that never changes.
- Invented "people viewing now" counters. Real traffic counters can be fair; made-up ones are not.
- An "expires tonight" bonus that expires every night.
- Repeated "final hours" emails. Use "last chance" once; use it twice and it means nothing.

## Why faking is expensive

Buyers share screenshots and threads. One posted example of a timer that reset can become the first thing people see when they search your name. This skill has no figure for how much faked pressure wins on the day; the cost to expect is lost trust later. If you feel you must fake it, the real problem is a weak value equation, so go back to value-equation.md.

## When you do not need it

- Subscriptions that can be cancelled any time.
- Small impulse purchases, where the pause is short and a limit feels forced.
- Luxury positioning, where limits are implied and stating them reads as low status.
- Audiences that already trust you and plan to buy.

A forced limit is worse than none.

## Diagnostic

1. What real constraint do you have: capacity, cohort, stock, season, batch?
2. Say it in plain words.
3. Can the buyer verify it, for instance by seeing the seat count?
4. Will you enforce it when it hits: close the door, raise the price, pull the bonus?
5. Is it shown next to the buy button rather than hidden?

Every business has some real limit (hours, calendar, batch size, attention). If you truly find none, do not add one.

## Order of work

1. Clear, specific outcome.
2. Belief: proof, method, guarantee.
3. Shorter wait.
4. Lower work and risk.
5. Only then a deadline or limit.

Scarcity closes a good offer. On a weak offer it only makes the buyer say no faster.
