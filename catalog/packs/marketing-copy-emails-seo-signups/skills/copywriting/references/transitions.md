# Transitions that guide a reader

Signposting helps people skim and helps search engines read the structure. Choose by function.

## By function
- Preview: "Here is what this covers", "We start with X, then Y", "The sections below explain".
- New topic: "When it comes to X", "Another factor is", "Now for X".
- Look back: "As noted above", "Building on that", "Back to X".
- Move on: "Next up", "With that covered, on to", "That brings us to".
- Add: "Also", "Plus", "On top of that", "There is also", "Beyond that".
- Contrast: "But", "That said", "On the flip side", "Unlike X, Y", "Despite this".
- Likeness: "Similarly", "Likewise", "The same goes for".
- Cause and result: "So", "This means", "That is why", "As a result", "Here is what happens".
- Example: "For example", "Take X", "Say you want to", "Consider this".
- Emphasis: "The key point", "What matters most", "The bottom line".
- Evidence: "According to {source}", "{Source} reports", "A study by {source} found", "{Expert} explains". Name a source only when a real one exists.
- Support: "The numbers confirm", "This fits findings from".
- Summary: "In short", "To recap", "The short version".
- Conclusion: "Wrapping up", "Here is what to do next", "Ready to start?".
- Questions (good for a spoken tone and snippet-style answers): "So what does this mean for you?", "What is the catch?", "Where do you start?".
- Before a list: "Here is how", "Follow these steps", "The main factors are".
- Soft claims, when something is not certain: may, might, could, tends to, often, usually, in most cases, evidence suggests.

## Habits
1. Match the audience: B2B can be a little more formal, consumer copy more chatty.
2. Vary the phrase; repeats get noticed.
3. Do not signpost every sentence; trust the reader.
4. Open paragraphs with the signal, so skimmers can navigate.
5. Read it aloud; if it sounds forced, simplify.
6. Put the important word early in the phrase.
7. Use "moreover" and "furthermore" rarely, and avoid "In conclusion" as a paragraph opener.

## Phrases that read as machine-written
Watch for stock openers such as "Let's delve into", "This begs the question", "In today's digital landscape", "At its core", and the hedge-everything pair "It is worth noting that" and "That being said". A "realm of" in a sentence is another sign. Replace each with a plain statement of the point.
