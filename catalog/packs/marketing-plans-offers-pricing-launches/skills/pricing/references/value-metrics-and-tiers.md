# Value metrics, tiers and personas

All prices below are invented examples.

## Common value metrics

| Unit charged | Fits | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Seat | Tools used by teams together | Customers share logins |
| Usage (calls, minutes, gigabytes) | Variable consumption | Bills feel unpredictable |
| Feature or add-on | Modular products | Too many add-ons confuse |
| Contact or record | Lists, CRMs, mailing tools | Cleaning data to dodge the limit |
| Transaction | Payments, marketplaces | Margin pressure on big sellers |
| Flat fee | Simple products | No growth as the customer grows |

## How many tiers

- Two: a clear small-versus-large split. Simple, but may leave money unclaimed.
- Three: the usual default. Entry gets people in, the middle plan is the one you want chosen, the top plan catches heavy users.
- Four or more: only for a wide range of customer sizes. Risk of choice paralysis.

## Differentiating tiers

- Gated features: basics everywhere, advanced items higher up. Works when features differ clearly in value.
- Usage limits: same features, higher limits above. Works when value grows with volume.
- Support: email, then priority, then a named contact. Suits products that need setup help.
- Access: API, single sign-on, branding. Suits large accounts.

## Example structure (invented prices)

| | Basic, 12 per month | Plus, 35 per month | Max, 90 per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seats | 3 | 15 | Unlimited |
| Projects | 5 | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Storage | 2 GB | 40 GB | 400 GB |
| Reports | Standard | Advanced | Custom |
| Support | Email | Priority | Named contact |
| API | No | Yes | Yes |
| Single sign-on | No | No | Yes |

The top plan sits at about 2.6x the middle one, inside the 2-3x starting point used in this skill (tune it to your customers).

## Packaging for personas

Buyers differ in willingness to pay, needs, buying process and how they judge value. Segment by company size, use case, sophistication and industry.

1. Define the personas, each with size, main need and rough willingness to pay (invented example: solo freelancer, small team of 2-10, growing company of 10-50, large account of 50+ with a custom quote).
2. Lay out a grid of features against personas and mark what each gets in full, in part, or not at all.
3. Price each persona against the value it receives, using research per segment. Charge enough to capture value without blocking adoption. Consider a separate landing page per segment.
